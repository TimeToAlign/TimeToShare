Drawing a TimeToShare manifest#

This specification states how one TimeToShare manifest is drawn: the graphical object of style rules (section 2), the arithmetic of positions (section 3), the default style (section 4), the fonts that measure every text (section 5), the layout that turns a manifest into a drawing (section 6), and the writers that serialize a drawing as SVG and TikZ text (section 7). Two implementations that follow it draw the same manifest, with the same inputs, as the same SVG and TikZ text, byte for byte.

A drawing is derived and never stored: there is no drawing schema, and no JSON form of a drawing is exchanged. This specification pins the drawing.

Sections 1 to 7 specify the drawing of one timeline, called the root, with all its descendants: a member drawing. Section 8 specifies the block that holds member drawings. Every manifest is drawn as one block, whether it holds one timeline or many, groups or none: its groups as frames and its ungrouped roots as cards, and the virtual timelines it places as cards too (section 8.10). Terms in code are JSON members.

The block draws the bundle, a frame a group, a card a timeline drawn as a root, and a band a facet of a timeline (README, “Containers”); these four containers are the boxes of a drawing.

1. Inputs and output#

draw(manifest, placement, layers, metrics, reading) returns the manifest’s drawing.

  • manifest: a TimeToShare manifest, read in its complete form, every key the manifest leaves out taking its default (README, “Defaults”), and in canonical order (README, “Canonical order”), whatever form and order it was written in. Its graphical object holds the manifest’s own style rules and its order (section 2). The drawing reads the manifest alone, never a table that its resources name.

  • placement: chosen by whoever places the drawing, such as an editor’s canvas or a script:

    • length, the graphical length L of every item of the block without a length of its own (section 8.6), in canvas units (default 400): the distance from a root’s start to its end;

    • spacing, the block’s spacing, the gap in canvas units between its items that lie at their default y (section 8.6), a finite number ≥ 0 (default 8);

    • margin, the block’s margin, empty space in canvas units drawn above its first item and below its last (section 8.4), a finite number ≥ 0 (default 0). A frame’s and a card’s spacing and margin are entries of places (section 8.6); none multiplies another;

    • width and height, the requested block size in canvas units, or null (the default) for the content’s own size;

    • places, the places of the block’s items and the sizes of its members’ cards (section 8.6): an object keyed by the ID of a group (its frame), by the ID of an ungrouped root (its card) and by the ID of a virtual timeline that is a member of no group (its card, which is drawn only where places holds its ID, section 8.10), each value an object with any of the members x, y, length, scale, spacing, margin, width and height; a group’s value may also hold cards, an object keyed by the ID of a member T, whose value states the width, height, spacing and margin of T’s card in that group’s frame, so that places[G].cards[T] is the card of member T in the frame of group G (default {});

    • arrangement, the block’s arrangement (section 8.6): null (the default), each item lying at its place, or an object {direction, order} that places the items in an order instead: direction is "vertical", the items one under another, or "horizontal", the items side by side in one row, every root that groups hang on drawn once behind the row (section 8.8, Drawn once); order is a list of the IDs of groups, ungrouped roots and placed virtual timelines, the order to take the items in. Like every input here, an arrangement says where the drawing places things and is never part of the manifest;

    • view, the view {width, height}, the screen area the drawing is meant for, in canvas units at the scale 1 (CSS pixels), each a finite number > 0 (default {width: 1280, height: 720}). It sets the proportion of the elisions a card derives where its elision-proportion is auto (section 8.9) and nothing else: an editor passes the area of its canvas, a script its own or the default, and the manifest stores nothing of it, so one manifest drawn for two views may differ in the widths of its derived elisions.

  • layers: the rule lists that style the drawing, from the lowest to the highest precedence: the defaults of section 4, then any lists an application supplies below the manifest (such as an editor’s settings), then the manifest’s own graphical object. No layer lies above the manifest: a drawing’s own styling is its manifest’s. Section 2 defines them.

  • metrics: the advance widths of the fonts of section 5.1.

  • reading: the reading in force of flow control, the flow-control elements the drawing counts as read (section 6.4, Flow control): null (the default), or an object {believer, beliefs}, believer the IRI of an actor and beliefs a non-empty list of belief states, a belief filter of the README (“Provenance and beliefs”). With an object, an element is read where its state for believer is one of beliefs, the state taken and the actors compared as the README’s belief filter takes and compares them; with null, an element is read where no actor believes it false (README, “Flow control”). An element the reading does not read is unread, and the drawing applies to unread elements what the README’s flow control says of rejected ones: no destination lands on an unread element, a to naming one names nothing (section 3.1), and an unread element is drawn faded (section 6.4, Flow control). With null, unread and rejected are one. An editor passes its user and the belief states it keeps (the user’s own beliefs deciding where the user holds one, the workspace’s belief filter elsewhere), a script its own or null; like placement, a reading is never part of the manifest.

The drawing is a block of width W and height H and a list of drawn elements in block-local canvas units, origin at the block’s top-left corner, y downwards, and the block-local position (Ox, Oy) of the place origin, from which places are measured (section 8.6). Every element carries its address (section 2.2) and, where it shows a coordinate, the exact coordinate it shows, whatever its label’s text (section 5.2). A drawing of an arranged block also states its arrangement: its direction, the order of its items (for horizontal, the row) and the roots drawn once, top to bottom, or, where horizontal is refused, the conflict, its groups, its roots and its reason (section 8.8, Drawn once). Canvas units become CSS pixels in SVG and points in TikZ (section 7).

A drawing does not depend on its position on a canvas, nor on its scale: a canvas or a writer places the block at a position and scales it uniformly by a scale factor z (section 7.1). The three ways of resizing a drawing change one input each:

Resize

Input changed

Effect

Scale

scale z

everything scales, proportions kept, text included

Box

width, height

only the block changes; the content keeps its place at the top left; the block never becomes smaller than the content (section 8.4)

Stretch

length (horizontal), spacing, or margin for a container with exactly one content (vertical), both (a corner); width, height where a block size is requested

horizontally, distances along time stretch; vertically, the gaps between the container’s contents grow or shrink, or, in a container with exactly one content, the equal margins above and below it; markers, line widths and text keep their size; the block follows the content (below)

On a stretch, a requested block size larger than the content keeps its proportion to the content, so the block follows the stretched content and the room the request adds stretches with it: width (or height) becomes the request times the stretched content’s size divided by the content’s size before the stretch, multiplied first, in binary64 (an editor may round the result to the grid it stores numbers on). A request no larger than the content before the stretch becomes null, and null stays null. A horizontal Stretch of the block scales every x, length and width that places states by the ratio of the lengths, and keeps every scale.

A container’s contents are a block’s items, a frame’s cards, a card’s root row, the root’s present bands (section 6.4) and its ancestor bands (section 8.3), none, one or two. A vertical Stretch of a container with two or more contents changes its spacing; with exactly one it changes its margin instead, the gaps being none, so that the container grows or shrinks by equal margins above and below its contents (sections 6.5 and 8.4), and its spacing is unchanged; with none it changes its spacing. Scale, Box and the horizontal Stretch keep every margin. A vertical Stretch of the block so changes its spacing or its margin alone: it moves the items at their default y and keeps every stated y and height.

Every frame and card of the block is resized in the same three ways, changing entries of places only (section 8.6). An item, a group’s frame or an ungrouped root’s card: Scale changes its scale, Box its width and height, Stretch its length (horizontal), its spacing or margin (vertical, as above) or both (a corner), its width and height following the stretched content as the block’s do. The card of a member T in the frame of a group G: Scale and the horizontal Stretch change G’s scale and length, since the members of a group share them; the vertical Stretch changes the card’s own spacing, or its own margin when the card has exactly one content, and Box its own width and height, the entry places[G].cards[T], so the cards of the same member in other groups’ frames keep theirs. An item’s or card’s content, for this rule, is its frame or card without its own request (for a member’s card, without its frame’s request too, since the frame’s width widens its cards), in item coordinates: for width its right edge less its left (section 8.2), for height its height of section 8.4. Every requested width or height of a frame or card whose content a Stretch of an item or a member’s card changes keeps its proportion to that content by the rule above: a horizontal Stretch of a group, from its frame or a member’s card, rescales every requested width of the frame and of its members’ cards, so the members’ lines keep one length and the room every request adds stretches with them; a vertical Stretch of a frame, which changes only the gaps between its cards or the margins around its one card, rescales its own requested height, and of a card, which changes its gaps or its margins, its own and its frame’s.

2. The graphical object#

"graphical": {
  "styles": {
    "highlight": {"stroke": "#7F00FF", "fill": "#7F00FF"}
  },
  "rules": [
    {"select": {"element": "label"}, "set": {"fill": "#404040"}},
    {"select": {"element": "wedge", "within": "#performance"}, "set": "highlight"},
    {"select": {"addresses": [{"id": "#region_1"}]}, "set": {"display": "none"}}
  ]
}

The object is optional; an absent graphical draws with the other layers alone. styles maps a style name to a declaration, an object of properties (section 2.4) and their values. rules is a list of rules, each a selector (select, section 2.3) and either a declaration or the name of a style in the same layer’s styles (set). The order of the rules matters, so writers never reorder them. Every layer of section 1 has this shape. The manifest’s own graphical also holds order, timestamps, matchstamps and elisions, which no other layer holds. order maps a group’s ID to the display order of the group’s members (section 8.1) and the ID of a timeline or of a virtual timeline to its band order (section 6.4).

timestamps is a list of named timestamps (default []), each an object {id, timeline, coordinate, name, display?, matchstamp?, groupstamp?} naming one position of one timeline: id is its ID (README, “Identity”), timeline the ID of a timeline of the manifest at any depth, a segment included, or of a virtual timeline (section 8.10), coordinate the position on that timeline’s own axis, a number as the timeline’s number type selects (README, “Values on a timeline’s own axis”), at least the zero of that number type and at most the timeline’s length when the length is a value, and name its name. A match timestamp, one with matchstamp: true (default false), may leave out its name. A groupstamp, one with groupstamp: true (default false), lies on a timeline that lies in a group: the timeline or one of its ancestors is listed in the group’s members, so that it is drawn in the group’s frame, as a member or inside a member’s card; a virtual timeline lies in the groups it is a member of. An ancestor of a member lies in no group through it. The groupstamp’s line extends across the frame of each group its timeline lies in, from the upper border of the topmost member card drawn there to the lower border of the bottommost (section 6.6, Cross-section lines); the members’ coordinates at that line are derived from the group and never stored. A match timestamp may be a groupstamp too. Their canonical order is by timeline, compared by Unicode code point, then by coordinate, compared by exact value, then by id, compared by code point.

A named timestamp’s coordinates in a card are what its cross-section lines cross there (section 6.6): a coordinate on every timeline drawn in the card whose line, as a root, a child or an ancestor line (section 8.3), spans the line’s x, its ends included, and on every map drawn there that spans it, each read on that timeline’s or map’s own axis; a segment without a line of its own (section 6.4) is crossed where its segment line is crossed within the segment’s span. Its primary coordinate is its coordinate on its timeline, the one its wedge and its labels mark; every other is a secondary coordinate, a groupstamp’s coordinates on the other members of its groups among them. The primary coordinates of a named MatchStamp are those of the match timestamps its alignment anchors name, the two of a MatchClaim to each anchor, and its secondary coordinates are theirs. An application that lists a timestamp’s coordinates, in a flag or a table, lists all of them, from the top of the card down, and highlights the primary one.

Timestamps, groupstamps and match timestamps are drawn by one rule, whether a timestamp is named on a root, on a child or on an ancestor of a member: each draws what its display lists (below; a match timestamp by default its cross-section line alone), its cross-section line across the whole of every card that draws its timeline, as a line or as an ancestor line, and its timeline decides only where its primary coordinate’s wedge and labels stand: in that timeline’s markers band (section 6.4, Markers), or beside its ancestor line (section 8.3). A secondary coordinate on a timeline is drawn as the primary one is, by a wedge and the labels the timestamp’s display lists, in that timeline’s markers band or beside its ancestor line, its coordinate label read on that timeline’s axis and written in that timeline’s number type: on an int axis the integer nearest the exact value, ties to even, on a float axis the double nearest it, and on a fraction axis the exact value (section 5.2); a map has no markers band, and a coordinate on a map is listed but draws no wedge. The words of display govern the elements of the secondary coordinates as they govern the primary one’s. The defaults hide every element of a secondary coordinate and turn its wedge −45 (section 4); a rule selecting them by the selector member coordinates (section 2.3) shows them.

A groupstamp’s coordinates on the other members of its groups are read at one proportion of each member’s drawn length (section 6.6), and only a known length gives a value there: one member’s length is never carried to another. So where the line crosses the card of a member whose length is unknown, it gives that member, and every timeline its card draws, a coordinate without a value; and where the member holding the timestamp’s own timeline has an unknown length, the proportion is one of its reach, and the line gives every other member, and every timeline their cards draw, a coordinate without a value. A coordinate without a value is drawn faded and without its coordinate label (section 6.6), and an application listing the coordinates lists it, in its place, with no value.

A named timestamp draws up to five kinds of element, each named by one word of its display: wedge, its coordinate marker, at its primary coordinate and at each secondary one (above); name, id and coordinate, its three labels, stacked at the thick end of each wedge (section 6.4, Markers): its name, its ID, and the coordinate the wedge marks in that timeline’s unit (section 5.2); and cross_section, its cross-section line, a vertical line across each card its timeline is drawn in, from the card’s upper border to its lower border, with a gap where the wedge and the labels of its primary coordinate stand (section 6.6). display is an array of distinct words from these five, listing what the timestamp draws; when it is absent the timestamp draws ["wedge", "name", "cross_section"], a match timestamp ["cross_section"], and an empty array draws nothing. A timestamp without a name draws no name label. Its order means nothing: writers write it in the canonical order wedge, name, id, coordinate, cross_section. An element whose word display does not list is not displayed, whatever the cascade sets (section 2.4).

matchstamps is a list of named MatchStamps (default []), each an object {id, name, alignment_anchors, match_claims} (README, “Named MatchStamps”). Each alignment anchor of alignment_anchors is an object {id, timestamps, display?}: timestamps names two match timestamps, and display lists what the anchor draws, distinct words from line, its alignment_anchor element, and name, the MatchStamp’s name as a label at the middle of that line (section 8.7); when it is absent the anchor draws ["line"]. Writers write it in the canonical order line, name. match_claims is never drawn. A match timestamp’s cross-section line is an ordinary cross-section line (section 6.6).

The drawing draws the named MatchStamps as the manifest stores them, and the SVG and TikZ writers write them so. What an editor derives from a MatchClaim table, such as the potential MatchStamps it shows in grey or the anchors of a named MatchStamp that the current table no longer gives, which it marks in red, is the editor’s own and never part of a drawing.

elisions is a list of elisions (default []), each an object {id, timeline, start, end, name?, width?, display?} that leaves a stretch of one timeline out of the drawing: every row drawn across the stretch stops where the stretch starts and resumes where it ends, at full scale on either side, and a narrow gap lies between, holding one ellipsis, centred in it, with the elision’s labels below it (sections 6.6 and 8.9). id is its ID (README, “Identity”); timeline names the timeline the elision lies on, at any depth, a segment included, or a virtual timeline (section 8.10), with the meaning and the validation of a named timestamp’s timeline; start and end, start < end, are its elided coordinates, positions on that timeline’s own axis, from its zero to its length (and only from its zero when the length is null), each a number as the timeline’s number type selects (README, “Values on a timeline’s own axis”). An elision moves with its timeline when that timeline’s offset changes. Nothing refers to an elision but an address in a rule (section 2.2), and no timeline lists its elisions. An elision is a container: it leaves out what lies on its stretch by position (section 2.4), so a child added inside the stretch later is elided without any edit. name is its name, a string its name label shows; an elision without one is labelled with the name of the timeline it lies on (section 5.3). Its natural width is the width its stretch has at full scale (section 3.2), and width sets the gap’s drawn width in exactly one of four forms:

width

The gap’s drawn width

absent

the standard gap of 32 canvas units

{"ratio": q}, 0 < q ≤ 1

q times its natural width

{"span": v}, v > 0

the width of v units of its timeline at full scale

{"canvas": c}, c > 0

c canvas units

A width larger than the natural width is drawn at the natural width: an elision never widens its stretch. display lists what the elision draws besides its gap, distinct words from ellipsis, its ellipsis, name, id and coordinate, its three labels, stacked as a named timestamp’s are, and continuation, grey continuations of the rows across the gap; when it is absent the elision draws ["ellipsis", "name"], an empty array draws a bare gap, and writers write it in the canonical order ellipsis, name, id, coordinate, continuation. Its order means nothing. Whatever display lists, the gap leaves the stretch out. Elisions may overlap each other and the span of a group member; neither is refused, and section 8.9 states what is drawn. Their canonical order is by timeline, compared by Unicode code point, then by start, compared by exact value, then by id, compared by code point.

An elision on a timeline that lies in a group, as a groupstamp’s timeline does (above), is a group elision of that group: it is drawn across the group’s frame, at one place in every member card, and carried into the ancestor cards behind the frame (section 8.9). Every other elision is a card elision, drawn in the cards of its timeline’s root that draw that root whole: an ungrouped root’s card and the ancestor cards behind frames (section 8.8), and for a virtual timeline, its own root, its placed card (section 8.10). Which card elisions a root’s cards draw, and whether they derive elisions of their own, the four elision properties of section 2.4 decide, in a rule addressed to the root’s cards (section 8.9). Whether an elision is a group elision is read from its timeline, never stored.

A reader that meets rules naming styles its styles does not hold issues one warning naming every missing style, and adds each to styles as an empty declaration; such a rule then sets nothing, and the graphical object is valid.

Every drawn thing is an element of one type, and so is every band of a timeline, which draws nothing of its own:

the block: the background of the drawing, holding everything drawn (section 8)

a group’s frame (section 8)

the raised container of a member drawing: one in the frame of each group that has the timeline as a member, or one for an ungrouped root or a placed virtual timeline in no group (sections 8 and 8.10)

ancestor_card

the card of a root that contains a member, behind the frame of that member’s group, at the proportions of the root’s axis; shown by default, hidden by a rule (section 8.8)

a coordinate marker: of a named timestamp of any timeline, at its primary coordinate or at a secondary one (section 2)

cross_section

the cross-section line of a named timestamp, a vertical line across each card its timeline is drawn in, as a line or as an ancestor line, from border to border, with a gap where the wedge and labels of its primary coordinate stand, and for a groupstamp also its lines across the frame of each group its timeline lies in (section 6.6)

alignment_anchor

the line of an alignment anchor of a named MatchStamp, the shortest line between the cross-section lines of its two timestamps (section 8.7)

elision

the gap of an elision: the narrow rectangle in which a stretch of a timeline is left out, from its card’s top border to its bottom border, or for a group elision across the frame’s member cards; it draws nothing unless a rule fills it, and its stroke draws the continuations of the rows that cross it (sections 6.6 and 8.9)

ellipsis

the ellipsis of an elision, three filled dots, one centred in each drawing of its gap (section 6.6)

name

the name of a row, in the name column, or the title of a group or of the manifest (section 8)

label

any other text: the name, ID or coordinate of a named timestamp, the name of a MatchStamp at an alignment anchor, the name, ID or elided coordinates of an elision, the label of a region, of a chunk, of an arc of a jump or of a segment, or the name, ID or label of a flow-control element (its stack, section 6.4, Flow labels)

A drawing has two kinds of mark at a position, and they are never confused. A coordinate marker marks a position of a timeline: it is a wedge, and the wedges of the named timestamps’ coordinates on every timeline, held by the markers band of that timeline or by its ancestor line, are the coordinate markers this version draws. A flow line is a break, a section break or a barline of a timeline’s flow_control, held by the flow control band and drawn as a vertical line; the markers band never holds one. A cross-section line is neither: it belongs to its named timestamp’s timeline and is held by that timeline’s line, never by its markers band nor by the timestamp’s wedge. Nor is a symbol: it shows how a flow-control element is notated, and marks no position of its own.

An element belongs to a timeline (section 2.2); the block, a frame, the titles of a group or of the manifest, and an alignment_anchor, which joins two timelines, with its label belong to none, and have no domain, modality or class. A card belongs to the timeline it holds, and so has its domain, modality and class. A band belongs to its timeline but has no domain and no modality, so no selector naming them matches it, and what it holds inherits through it its timeline’s values unless a rule matches the band itself (section 2.5). An element has the domain (graphical, logical or physical) and the modality (continuous or discrete) of its timeline’s unit. A map instead has the domain and modality of the map’s target_unit, so a map is drawn in the style of the timelines it converts to. A virtual timeline’s elements, a chunk’s brace and boundary included, take the domain, modality and class of the virtual timeline’s own unit, and those a switched card draws on its root line those of its timeline (section 8.10):

Domain

Units

logical

number, floating_measures, whole_notes, quarters, ticks

physical

milliseconds, seconds, minutes, samples, frames

graphical

pixels, meters, centimeters, millimeters, inches, points, css_pixels, user_units

ticks, samples, frames and pixels are discrete, every other unit, css_pixels and user_units included, continuous. A map whose target_unit is null has no domain and no modality, so no selector naming them matches it and it inherits its style through its band (section 2.5). An element’s class is the timeline class its timeline’s unit decides: Continuous or Discrete for the modality, then Logical, Physical or Graphical for the domain, then Timeline (seconds decides ContinuousPhysicalTimeline, ticks DiscreteLogicalTimeline). A manifest writes no class; the unit decides it. A map instead has the map’s type, one of the ten conversion-map types of the schema (LinearMap, TableMap, ConstantMap, PiecewiseMap, ChainMap, CombinationMap, MeasureRecordMap, MeasureNumberMap, FloatingMeasureMap, MeasureBeatMap). A band has no class; it has its facet instead: markers, conversion_maps, children, regions or flow_control.

Elements form a tree that carries inheritance (section 2.5): the block holds the timeline; a timeline or child holds its five bands, and each band what it holds: the markers band the wedges of the named timestamps’ coordinates on its timeline, primary and secondary (section 2), and their labels, each label beside its wedge and not held by it, the conversion maps band its maps, the children band its child timelines, the regions band its regions, and the flow control band its flow lines and jumps and the symbols of its jump targets; a flow line or a jump holds its symbol and its stack (section 6.4, Flow labels), a jump also its arcs’ labels and its fork, and a label or stack moved out of its first tier holds its leader (the first label of the stack); the flow control band of a timeline holds its timeline’s elements in this tree, for the cascade, wherever they are laid out (the root’s band lays out the flow control of its whole tree, section 6.4); a timeline or child also holds the cross-section lines of its timeline’s named timestamps and the gaps of the card elisions and derived elisions lying on its timeline (section 8.9), each gap holding its ellipsis and its labels, directly and in no band; any other element holds its own name and label where it has one (section 5.3; a map and a band have neither), except that a segment’s label and its boundary are held by its segment line, directly and in no band, as parts of that line (section 6.4). A segment is a child timeline of its segment line, held by its children band. One that is not a segment line is a node of the tree although no line of its own is drawn, holding its five bands, its named timestamps’ cross-section lines and the gaps of the card and derived elisions lying on it as a line does, whose content is drawn at its segment line’s line (section 6.4), and has no element type, so inside never names it; one that is a segment line is drawn as a child (section 6.1). The line of a virtual timeline, and the root line of a switched card, holds what a root’s line holds; its regions band holds the braces of its chunks with their labels, before any region, and the line holds the chunks’ boundaries directly, in no band, as a segment line holds its segments’ (section 8.10). Section 8 states the top of the tree: the block, frames, cards, ancestor cards and ancestor lines; a frame also holds the gaps of its group elisions; the block also holds every alignment_anchor, and an alignment_anchor holds its label.

2.2 Addresses#

Every element of a drawing carries an address: an object {id?, facet?, part?} built from the IDs of the manifest (README, “Identity”) and compared as a whole object. No address is a string composed from IDs. id is an element’s ID; facet is one of the five facets markers, conversion_maps, children, regions and flow_control, and names a band; part is a non-empty array of the words start, end, name, label, id, coordinate, card, line, elision, ellipsis, symbol, fork and leader, and names a part of an element, or begins with a chunk index, an integer from 0, and names a chunk drawn in a regions band (section 8.10); end names no element in this version. facet stands only with id, and with part only where the part begins with a chunk index. The name or label of an element is addressed by the element’s address with name or label appended to its part; a named timestamp has no row and so no name element, and the part name of its address names its first label, a label element like its other two. One rule addresses every element of every manifest, one timeline or many. This specification states IDs bare, as the data-id attributes of the SVG hold them; in a manifest file the id of an address, the within of a selector and every other ID reference are written # followed by the ID (README, “Written in a file”), as the snippets of section 2 show. So a rule selecting that address styles every drawing of it (section 2.5), and an application that selects or marks an element by its address marks every drawing of it.

2.3 Selectors#

A selector is an object with any of these members:

  • element: an element type;

  • addresses: a non-empty list of distinct addresses (section 2.2); it matches an element whose address equals one of them as a whole object, so {"id": "score"} matches the line of score and its ancestor lines, never its bands, cards or wedges. An address is open like every object: a member other than id, facet and part is an unknown key (README, “Unknown keys”), and the address holding it matches no element;

  • domain, modality: a domain, a modality;

  • class: a timeline class (section 2.1) or a conversion-map type, or the IRI of a class, compact or full, which matches every region whose type statement (README, “Describing a manifest”) names that class and no other element. The two IRIs are compared expanded: in a selector, of every layer, the prefix tts stands for the format’s namespace https://timetoalign.github.io/timetoshare/ns#, another compact IRI is expanded by the manifest’s @context, which must declare its prefix (the validators refuse it otherwise, iri), and a full IRI is itself; each value of a region’s type statement is expanded by the manifest’s @context (Python’s expand_iri, npm’s expandIri), a value it does not expand being itself. So {"element": "region", "class": "tts:AtomicSection"} matches the atomic sections (README, “Atomic sections”), which the defaults hide (section 4), and the rule

    {"select": {"element": "region", "class": "tts:AtomicSection"}, "set": {"display": "inline"}}

    in a manifest’s graphical shows them, as an editor’s toggle “Atomic sections”, in the properties of a regions band and of a flow control band, writes it and removes it. It is one rule, whichever band’s properties show the toggle: it shows every atomic section of the manifest, each held by the regions band of the timeline that holds it and drawn only where that band is displayed (section 2.4);

  • facet: a facet (section 2.1); it matches the bands drawing that facet;

  • within: a timeline ID; it matches the elements belonging to that timeline or to any of its descendants;

  • inside: an element type; it matches the elements held, at any depth, by an element of that type in the tree of section 2.1 (so {"element": "name", "inside": "ancestor"} matches the names of ancestor lines and no other name).

  • coordinates: primary or secondary; it matches the elements of a named timestamp’s primary coordinate, or those of its secondary coordinates (section 2): wedge elements and the label elements of their stacks, the parts name, id and coordinate. It matches no other element: not a cross-section line, which belongs to its timestamp as a whole and marks no one coordinate, nor an alignment anchor or its label, nor any element that is no named timestamp’s.

An element matches a selector when it matches every member present. The empty selector {} matches every element. A band is selected as every element is: {"element": "band"} matches every band, {"facet": "regions"} every regions band, {"addresses": [{"id": "score", "facet": "regions"}]} the regions band of score, and {"inside": "band"} everything that bands hold, the bands of every timeline but a member drawing’s root included, since a child is held by its parent’s children band. A timestamp’s coordinates are selected so too: {"element": "wedge", "coordinates": "secondary"} matches the wedge of every secondary coordinate of every named timestamp, and the rule

{"select": {"element": "wedge", "coordinates": "secondary"}, "set": {"display": "inline"}}

in a manifest’s graphical shows those wedges, which the defaults hide (section 4), still turned −45 by the defaults, since the rule outranks every default rule by its layer and sets no rotation (section 2.5); the same rule with the selector {"coordinates": "secondary"} shows their labels too, and one selecting {"element": "wedge", "coordinates": "primary"} restyles the own wedge of every timestamp at once.

  • An element hides its label. A named timestamp’s labels are not its wedge’s (section 2.1), and its wedge hides none of them; nor are a jump’s stack, symbol and fork its arcs’, nor a flow line’s symbol its line’s: a jump whose arcs are hidden, as the defaults hide them (section 4), still draws its symbol, its fork and its stack.

  • A label hides its leader. A leader (section 6.6, Leaders) is displayed only when the label it joins, or the first label of the stack it joins, is displayed.

  • A line hides its timeline’s whole drawing. A line that is not displayed, the root’s and a child’s alike, hides its name, the maps, boundaries and segment labels on it (section 6.4), the cross-section lines and the gaps it holds (section 2.1), and its five bands with everything they hold, at any depth, its children and its segments with everything of theirs included. It takes no room, its stroke included: a child whose line is not displayed is not placed and has no row (section 6.4, Children), and section 6.5 states what a member drawing whose root’s line is not displayed still draws.

  • A segment without a line hides its bands. A segment that is not a segment line has no line of its own (section 6.1); when it is not displayed, its five bands and everything they hold are not displayed either, nor are the cross-section lines it holds (section 2.1), so it adds nothing to its segment line’s bands (section 6.4, Segments without a line) and draws no cross-section line. The boundary and the label of a segment, of either kind, are parts of its segment line’s line (section 2.1): that line hides them, the segment never does.

  • An ancestor line hides its name and its timestamps. An ancestor line (section 8.3) that is not displayed hides its name and what it holds of named timestamps (section 8.1): the wedges and labels of the coordinates on it and the cross-section lines of its timeline’s timestamps in that card; it has no row.

  • An ancestor card hides its drawing. An ancestor card (section 8.8) that is not displayed draws nothing of its member drawing, takes no room, and the block does not grow for it.

  • An elision leaves out what lies on its stretch. A displayed gap (section 6.6, Elisions), of a card elision in its card or of a group elision across a frame and in the ancestor cards behind it, leaves out every element there that marks a position in the gap (section 3.2), whatever timeline holds it, and cuts every row that crosses it, whatever its display lists. A gap that is not displayed leaves out nothing: its stretch is drawn at full scale, as without the elision, and so is every stretch of an elision a card does not draw (section 8.9).

No other element hides another. A frame or card that is not displayed keeps everything drawn in it (section 8.4); a member’s line keeps its ancestor lines, which are not part of its timeline’s drawing; a named timestamp’s wedge, each of its labels and its cross-section line are displayed or hidden each on its own, so hiding a timeline’s markers band hides its timestamps’ wedges and labels and keeps their cross-section lines; an alignment anchor’s line and its label are displayed or hidden each on its own too, whatever the cross-section lines it joins show, though a timeline line that is not drawn gives an anchor no end there (section 8.7); and no band takes its display from another: hiding the band {"id": "score", "facet": "regions"} hides the regions of score alone, and the regions of a child of score are drawn, at the child’s own line, only when the child’s own regions band is displayed. An atomic section is a region (README, “Atomic sections”) and so is held by the regions band of the timeline that holds it, never by a flow control band, which neither holds nor lays out a region: hiding a root’s flow control band hides no atomic section, hiding the root’s regions band hides the root’s own atomic sections alone, and the atomic sections of a child, such as the movements of a work hold, are drawn only when the child’s own regions band, and its line, are displayed. The flow-control elements of a child are held by the child’s own flow control band (section 2.1) though the root’s band lays them out (section 6.4): hiding the child’s line or its flow control band hides them, and hiding the root’s flow control band hides everything it lays out, the flow control of the root’s whole tree in that card. The defaults hide no band (section 4).

2.5 The cascade#

The value of a property on an element is found per property:

  1. Of the rules matching the element that set this property, the highest-ranking sets it.

  2. When none sets it and the property is inherited, the element takes its parent’s value in the tree of section 2.1.

  3. Otherwise the property’s initial value applies: stroke none, stroke-width 1, stroke-dasharray none, fill none, opacity 1, font-size 9, rx 0, shadow none, display inline, content none, max-width none, rotation 0, elision-mode auto, elision-proportion auto, elision-depths auto, elision-display ellipsis name, label-display name label, and flow none, which no declaration writes: the card is drawn as written.

The rank of a rule is, from the most to the least significant:

  1. its layer: a rule of a later layer outranks every rule of an earlier one;

  2. whether its selector names addresses;

  3. the depth of its within: none is 0, the root 1, its children 2, and so on; deeper outranks shallower;

  4. whether its selector names class, facet or coordinates;

  5. whether its selector names element;

  6. whether its selector names inside;

  7. how many of domain and modality its selector names;

  8. its position: the later rule of one list wins.

The cascade runs on each drawing of an element at its own place in the tree. Drawings that share an address (section 2.2) match the same addresses, within and element, but each inherits from its own parent and matches inside by its own holders: the name of an ancestor line matches {"inside": "ancestor"}, the name of the same timeline’s row in a card that draws its line does not.

3. Numbers#

3.1 Positions on the root’s axis#

A position derived from time is a value on the root’s axis: a coordinate of a timeline is carried to the root by adding the offsets of its ancestors, from the root down. The root starts at the zero of its number type (0, or 0.0 for a float) and ends at its length; a child of a timeline starting at s, with offset o, starts at s + o and ends at that start plus its own length; a region, flow-control element or named timestamp of a timeline starting at s lies at s plus its coordinate. Each arc of a jump runs from the jump’s own position, its from, to its to, s plus the place of its destination on the timeline’s axis: for a to, the coordinate of the jump target, the start of the region or the offset of the child timeline it names, or 0 for the timeline itself; for a by, the jump’s coordinate plus by. For a destination without a place, the timeline played is the root of timelines whose tree holds the jump, whose flow control band draws that tree’s elements (section 6.4, Gathering), and the to is the place the README (“Flow control”) states, found on that root’s axis and, for a member that is a child timeline, carried to the member’s (section 8.3): A destination without a place lands at the nearest section break, not unread (section 1), before its Jump on the axis of the timeline played, among those of that timeline and of all its descendants, but never before the start of the timeline stating the Jump, which counts as a section boundary for it: where no such section break lies from that start up to the Jump, the destination lands at that start. A destination with by 0 draws no arc, nor does one whose to names nothing or names an unread element: as a to naming a rejected element names nothing (README, “Flow control”), a to naming an unread element names nothing to the drawing, so no arc is drawn to that element’s place. In a valid manifest only a rejected jump names a rejected element; under a reading, any jump may name an unread one. Where a length is null, section 3.4 says where the timeline ends. A virtual timeline drawn as a root is an axis of its own, its positions its own coordinates; where its chunks lie on it, and where a switched card draws its timeline’s elements, section 8.10 states, by this same arithmetic.

A coordinate’s value is read as its number type selects: a float value is a float, an int or fraction value is exact. Values combine as Python’s numeric tower combines them: the sum of two exact values is exact; a sum involving a float converts each exact operand to the nearest double (ties to even) and adds in IEEE 754 binary64 arithmetic, rounding to nearest, ties to even.

The midpoint of two positions a and b, on which section 6 centres a segment label, a region’s brace tip and label and the label of a jump’s arc, is (a + b) / 2 in the same arithmetic: the sum a + b combined as above, then halved, exactly when the sum is exact and as a binary64 division by 2 when it is a float. On a float axis it is therefore the double sum halved, not the exact midpoint of the two doubles rounded once.

The format and both validators accept a child whose offset is negative, which starts before its parent’s zero. The readers of timetoalign and TimeLineEditor refuse such a child as not supported yet, and this version of the specification does not draw it.

3.2 From the axis to the canvas#

A position t on the root’s axis, with λ the root’s length, or its reach when its length is null (section 3.4), becomes the proportion u = t / λ: an exact quotient when both are exact, then rounded once to the nearest double; a binary64 division when either is a float; 0 when λ is 0. Its x in item coordinates (section 8.2) is

x(t) = x0 + L × u

in binary64, x0 the start of the root’s line (section 8.2). This is the only step from an exact value to a double, at any depth of nesting. A label is spelled from the coordinate (section 5.2), never from the double.

The elided axis. A card that draws gaps (section 8.9: an ancestor card, an ungrouped root’s card, or a member’s card in a frame with group elisions) has an x-axis that is linear piece by piece: a sequence of runs, the stretches drawn at full scale, parted by gaps. Each gap lies over a stretch [a, b] of the root’s axis, a < b, no two gaps of a card overlap, and every gap comes from a stored elision or is derived by the card (section 8.9). A gap’s natural width is n = L × ub − L × ua, ua and ub the proportions of a and b, the products formed first; its drawn width g (section 8.9) is at most n, and its shrink is n − g. A position t lies in a gap when a < t < b, compared exactly: the ends of a gap lie in none, but in the runs on either side. A position in no gap has the x

x(t) = x0 + (L × u − D)

D being the sum of the shrinks of the gaps with b ≤ t, compared exactly, each shrink formed first and the shrinks summed from the left. Before the first gap D is 0 and x(t) is the value above, x0 + L × u; so every run keeps the scale L / λ of the card, a gap is drawn x(b) − x(a) wide, and every position right of a gap lies its shrink further left. The end of a root of unknown length (section 3.4) lies at x0 + (L − D), D the sum of every shrink. A position in a gap has no x: an element marking it is not drawn (section 6.6, Elisions). Wherever sections 6.7 and 8.2 write L × u for a drawn position, it stands here for L × u − D. Gaps move x alone: section 6 lays the card out as without them, its first fits (section 6.3) measuring every extent by L × u, so that the card’s rows, slots, widths and ordinates are those of the card drawn whole, and an element that is not drawn because it lies in a gap keeps its room; only the label stacks of elisions (section 6.4, Markers) are added to the card’s markers bands, each as high as the lines it reaches in its drawn tier, which the gaps’ x set (section 6.6, Elisions).

Seams. An ancestor card’s axis may also jump where it leaves nothing out. A seam at a position m of the root’s axis parts two runs: the run left of it ends at m and the run right of it starts at m, its width w ≥ 0 further right. On an axis of one scale, the card’s L / λ, a seam counts in D above as the shrink −w of every position right of it, so everything right of it lies w further right; where the runs on its two sides have scales of their own (section 8.8, Drawn once), the section placing it gives each run’s x. A position at m itself lies on the side the section placing the seam states (section 8.8). A seam is no gap and no elision: it lies in no gap, leaves out no stretch, has no address and draws no rectangle, ellipsis, continuation or label: it is no element of the drawing (section 1), and an application listing a drawing’s elisions lists none. A row that crosses a seam, starting before m and ending after it, compared exactly, is drawn across it whole, its line or outline running on from the one run to the other without a break; an element at a position other than m takes the x of the run it lies on. Seams lie where an ancestor card’s member lies behind its frame (section 8.8, step 2) and where an arrangement changes the scale of a root drawn once without leaving anything out (section 8.8, Drawn once).

The frame’s elided axis. The member drawings of a frame share x0 and L (section 8.2). A frame with group elisions (section 8.9) has one elided axis for all of them, over proportions of the frame: each of its gaps lies over a stretch [p, q] of proportions, 0 ≤ p < q ≤ 1, with the natural width n = L × q − L × p; a position of proportion u (its proportion of its member drawing’s root, section 3.2) lies in the gap when p < u < q, compared as doubles, and one in no gap has the x x0 + (L × u − D), D the sum of the shrinks of the gaps with q ≤ u, compared as doubles, summed as above. So a gap lies at one x in every member card of the frame, the rows of every card, their descendants and their ancestor lines read x through it, and the frame’s first fits are as without its gaps.

3.3 Canvas arithmetic#

Every other number of a drawing is a double, computed in IEEE 754 binary64 in the order its formula is written, left to right, multiplications and divisions before additions and subtractions, and a constant factor such as 4/3, 9.5w, 8w, 5.5w, 4w, 2w, 1.5w, 1.2w or 2.5w formed before it multiplies (1.2w is the double nearest the exact product of 6/5 and w, not the binary64 product of the double 1.2 and w). A distance outwards is summed whole, left to right as written and a label gap of 2 included, before it becomes an ordinate yl − o or yl + o, yl the ordinate of the line of the timeline whose band it lies in (section 6.4); the boundaries and segment labels of a line are offset from that line (section 6.4), the wedges and labels of the named timestamps on an ancestor line from that line (section 8.3), and the label of a flow-control element’s stack from the flow row (section 6.4). A label’s baseline is then that ordinate plus a(9) or minus d(9). max and min compare exactly.

3.4 Timelines of unknown length#

A timeline whose length is null has an unknown length: the manifest does not say where it ends, and the drawing never invents an end for it. A root of unknown length is scaled to its reach; the end of a timeline of unknown length is found by the rules below, and is marked as open where they say so.

The reach of a timeline T is a value on T’s own axis: the largest of the zero of T’s number type and of every coordinate its content reaches on that axis, which is

  • for each child at offset o, o plus the child’s length, or, when the child’s length is null, o plus the child’s own reach, recursively;

  • the start and the end of each region;

  • the coordinate of each element of flow_control, and the place each destination with a by reaches; every other to of an arc (section 3.1) is a coordinate counted here, a region’s start, a child’s offset or 0, so it adds nothing further;

  • the coordinate of each named timestamp of T (section 2).

Sums combine as section 3.1 combines values. The values compare exactly; of several equal largest values, an exact one (int or fraction) is taken before a float. Content counts whether or not it is displayed, so a style never changes a scale. Conversion maps are not content. A timeline without content, or whose content reaches no further than its start, has the reach 0. The reach is a coordinate on an axis; the distances that sections 6 and 8 call the reach of a map stack, a line, a band or a mark are canvas distances outwards from a line, and have nothing to do with it.

The end. Every drawn timeline T starting at S (section 3.1) ends at a position on the root’s axis, and its end is either known or open. The first of these rules that applies gives it:

  1. T’s length is a value: T ends at S plus its length; known.

  2. T is the root of its member drawing: it ends at λ, its reach; open.

  3. T is a segment followed by another segment, in canonical order: it ends where the next segment starts; known, since the segment line fixes it.

  4. T is the last segment of its segment line: it ends where its segment line ends; known or open as that end is.

  5. Otherwise: T ends at S plus its reach; open.

The reach does not follow these rules. A segment of unknown length that is followed by another counts towards its segment line’s reach as every child of unknown length does, with its offset plus its own reach, never with the end rule 3 gives it; so when its content runs past the next segment’s start, that content counts towards the reach all the same, and is drawn where it lies, past the segment’s end.

Xe, x of the end, is x of the end’s position (section 3.2), except for an end that is the root’s under rule 2, directly or through rule 4, where Xe = x0 + L whatever the reach. So the reach of a root of unknown length lies at the end of the graphical length, and a root whose reach is 0 is still drawn L long, every position of it at u = 0 (section 3.2). A line whose end is open ends in three chevrons (section 6.6), never in a mark that would assert a position.

  • A float is rounded to three decimal places as Python’s round(x, 3) rounds it: the exact value of the double to the nearest multiple of 1/1000, ties to even, the result being the double nearest that multiple.   • Any other exact value is a fraction n/d in lowest terms. When d is at most 2520 (the least common multiple of 1 to 10) it is written mixed: its integer part when that is not 0, then, when a remainder r is left, r/d, with a space between the two when both are written. So 219/16 is written 13 11/16, −3/8 -3/8, 3 3 and 1000 1000: the digits of a fraction are never grouped. When d is greater than 2520 and has no prime factor other than 2 and 5, the value is written as its decimal, which ends, with every place it needs (1/4096 is 0.000244140625). Otherwise it is written ≈, a space, and the value rounded to twelve decimal places, ties to even, with all twelve places: 1/2521 is ≈ 0.000396667989 and −1/2550 ≈ -0.000392156863, the ≈ before the sign.

A value in seconds is written as a clock. Its magnitude, rounded as above (a float to three places, an approximated fraction to twelve), is split into whole hours, whole minutes and the seconds that remain, written {hours}h{minutes}′{seconds}″ with the prime ′ (U+2032) and the double prime ″ (U+2033): the hours only when they are not 0, the minutes when they are not 0 or follow hours, the seconds always, and each part that follows a written part with its integer part padded to two digits. The seconds are written as the value is. Those of a float are the decimal its repr spells, with only the places they need and none when they are whole: 312.48979591836735 is labelled 5′12.49″, 42.5 42.5″, 75.0 1′15″, 3725.0 1h02′05″, 3599.9996 1h00′00″ and 1e-05 0″. 5.3 Names and labels#

The name of a timeline is its name, or, when name is null, its whole id, which is never parsed (original_score:dgt_1 is named original_score:dgt_1). A conversion map has no name or label in the drawing. A region’s label is its name, and a region whose name is null has none; the label of each arc of a jump is its destination’s label, never that destination’s manifestation, which its symbol shows (section 6.4, Symbols), nor the jump’s own label, and an arc whose destination’s label is null or absent has none; a flow line or a jump has a stack of up to three labels, its name, its whole id and its own label, each where it is not null, of which it draws those its label-display lists (section 6.4, Flow labels), so that the jump’s own label and name label the jump once, never each of its arcs; a jump target draws no label; the text of a branch of a fork is its destination’s passes as printed (section 6.4, Forks); a segment’s label is its name, and a segment whose name is null has none; a named timestamp has three labels, its name, its whole id, and its coordinate, labelled as section 5.2 labels a coordinate, in its timeline’s unit, and draws those its display lists (section 2). An elision has three labels: its name label, its name, or the name of the timeline it lies on where it has none (an elision a card derives always the latter); its id label, its whole id (a derived elision has none, so it draws no id label); and its coordinate label, the pretty text (section 5.2) of the start of its gap’s elided coordinates without its pretty unit, – (U+2013) and the pretty text of their end, both on that timeline’s own axis, so 1–31 ♩ and 600–1 754 px; in seconds both values are clocks, 55.5″–1′30″. The start and end are those of the gap as drawn (section 8.9): a clipped elision shows the stretch of its piece, a derived one the coordinates its gap leaves out. The title of a group, or of the manifest, is its name, or, when name is null, its whole id. A manifest with neither a name nor an id, an anonymous bundle, has no title.

6. Layout#

6.1 Traversal#

The traversal lists the timelines in pre-order: the root, then each child in canonical order (by offset, compared by exact value, then by ID, compared by Unicode code point, whatever the order of the children keys), each followed by its own descendants.

A timeline whose is_segment_line is true is a segment line; its children are its segments, contiguous from its start to its end. Every segment lies on its segment line’s line, which shows where it starts by a boundary and its name by a label (section 6.4), both hidden with that line (section 2.4). A segment that is not a segment line has no line of its own: wherever this section speaks of its line, it is its segment line’s line. A segment that is itself a segment line is drawn as a child line in its segment line’s children band (section 6.4, Children), and its own segments lie on that line; neighbouring segments drawn so meet end to start, the start circle of one inside the open diamond of the other (section 6.6).

6.3 First fit#

Several steps below place items one at a time, in a given order, into numbered rows (or tiers) 1, 2, 3, …: an item goes to the lowest row holding no item whose extent conflicts with its own. Extents are x intervals measured from the root’s start, at any depth of the member drawing, each end computed as L × u (section 3.2) and never as a difference x − x0, so no row depends on x0; an extent named by positions below runs from L × u of the one to L × u of the other. [a1, b1] and [a2, b2] conflict when a1 < b2 and a2 < b1, or when a1 = a2. So an item that starts where another ends shares its row, and two items starting at the same point never do. The extent of a label is [left − 1, right + 1], so labels keep the label separation apart: for a label of width wi centred on a position of proportion u, [L × u − wi / 2 − 1, L × u + wi / 2 + 1], and for the stack of a flow-control element, wi the width of its widest label, [L × u + 3 − 1, L × u + 3 + wi + 1], each end computed left to right. Every band fills its own rows.

6.4 Levels and bands#

Every timeline’s bands. Every timeline drawn with a line of its own, the root and every child at any depth alike, has its five bands, one for each of its facets (section 2.1), at its own line, and this section lays them out for each such timeline whose line is displayed (section 2.4) as for the root: a band’s timeline is the timeline it belongs to, and “its line”, “above” and “below” are that timeline’s. yl is the ordinate of a timeline’s line: y_root for the root (section 6.5), and for a child the ordinate its row gives it (Children). A segment that is not a segment line has its bands at its segment line’s line (Segments without a line).

The band order. The band order of a timeline lists line, its own line, and its five facets, each standing for the band drawing it, from top to bottom. A timeline without an entry in the manifest’s graphical.order (section 2; README, “Groups and the graphical object”) follows its inherited order: its parent’s band order, and for a root of timelines the default band order of section 4. A member that is a child timeline follows its parent so too. Changing a timeline’s band order therefore changes that of every descendant without an entry of its own. An entry lists line and any of the facets; it is completed against the inherited order: the listed entries keep their order, and every facet it leaves out keeps the side of line it has in the inherited order and lies beyond the listed entries on that side, in the inherited order’s sequence, the facets left out above the line before the listed entries and those below after them. For a root, ["markers", "line"] is so ["children", "conversion_maps", "markers", "line", "regions", "flow_control"], ["regions", "line"] is ["children", "conversion_maps", "regions", "line", "markers", "flow_control"], and ["line"] the default; a child of that first root stating ["line", "children"] has ["conversion_maps", "markers", "line", "children", "regions", "flow_control"]. The bands listed before line lie above its line, those after it below: up and down, whichever side of its parent’s line it lies on.

What a band holds. A band draws what its own timeline holds of its facet (section 2.1), and what another timeline holds only where a segment without a line of its own joins its segment line’s bands (below), and in the flow control band of the root (below):

  • the markers band its coordinate markers (Markers): the wedges and labels of its named timestamps (section 2), and the label stacks of the elisions lying on its timeline, never their cross-section lines, which its timeline’s line holds (section 2.1); no timeline’s start or end is marked in it, and a flow line is drawn by the flow control band, never by the markers band (section 2.1), so the markers band of a timeline that draws no wedge and no label of a named timestamp or an elision draws nothing and is absent (Levels);

  • the conversion maps band its maps (Conversion maps);

  • the children band its children (Children);

  • the regions band its regions (Regions), and, on the line of a virtual timeline or the root line of a switched card, before them the braces of the chunks of its FlowMap and the labels of its gaps (section 8.10);

  • the flow control band of the root, the flow control of the whole tree (Flow control): its symbols, flow lines, forks and the arcs of its jumps, with their labels and stacks; the flow control band of any other timeline draws nothing and is never present.

Gathering. Flow control holds across descendants, so the root’s flow control band draws the flow-control elements of the root and of every descendant, at any depth, segments included, each at its position on the root’s axis (section 3.1), whichever timeline states it; no other timeline draws a flow control band. Each element keeps its place in the tree of section 2.1, held by the flow control band of the timeline that states it, so that the cascade, within and the hiding of section 2.4 treat it as that timeline’s: a child whose line or flow control band is not displayed draws none of its elements, and they take no room. The root’s flow control band is present when it is displayed and draws an element, of the root’s or of a descendant’s; when it is not displayed, it draws nothing of the whole tree it lays out and is absent (section 2.4). When the root of the member drawing is a member that is a child timeline (section 8.1), its flow control band draws, besides the elements of its own tree, the elements of every other timeline of the tree of its top-level root, the root of timelines above it, whose position on the member’s axis (section 8.3: an ancestor’s coordinate plus ts, compared exactly) lies from 0 to λ, both included, λ the member’s length or reach (section 3.4); those of the elements that lie outside the member’s own tree are held by the member’s flow control band. So a section of a movement drawn as a member shows the repeats, the voltas and the jumps of the movement and of the work that lie in its stretch. An arc is drawn in such a band only where its to lies in that stretch too.

An element that is not displayed (section 2.4) is drawn in no band and takes no room.

Levels. A timeline’s line lies at level 0. A band is present when it draws an element, and in a row of one band order also when the band of its facet of another line of the row is (Children); the present bands above the line are the levels 1, 2, … outwards, level 1 being the present band listed nearest before line in its band order, and those below it the levels −1, −2, …, level −1 being the present band listed nearest after it. An absent band has no level and takes no room. A distance o outwards from a timeline’s line lies at yl − o above and yl + o below.

Where a band lies. Each band is laid out from its start c, a distance outwards from its timeline’s line, so that a band starting at c = 0 lies against the line; the paragraphs below place its content from c. A band’s width is the distance its content reaches outwards from c, and its outer edge is c plus its width, the width summed first. The band at level 1, and the one at level −1, starts

  • at c = 0 when it is the markers band or the conversion maps band, each of which lies against the line and shares its room with the line’s own reach (Level 0, below): its outer edge is then the larger of c plus its width and the line’s reach on that side;

  • otherwise at S beyond the larger of the line’s reach on that side and w / 2, w the line’s stroke width, so that its content keeps the gap S from the line’s stroke even where the line reaches nothing.

Each further band starts at the outer edge of the band at the level before it plus S. A children band’s own start is instead the larger of the start these rules give it and pl / 2, wherever it lies, pl the row pitch of its timeline, the pitch of the row its line lies in (section 6.2; p0 for the root), so that the rows of a side, its timeline’s included, lie one pitch apart when nothing widens them; the band starts as Children lays it out, together with the children bands of the other lines of that row. A conversion maps band’s own start is the start these rules give it, and the band starts as Conversion maps lays it out, together with the conversion maps bands of the other lines of its row. The markers band and the conversion maps band are two bands, never over each other: on one side they lie at two levels, the outer starting at least S beyond the outer edge of the inner. The outer edge of a side of a timeline is that of its outermost band there, or, when no band is present there, the line’s reach on that side.

The order of the starts. A band’s own start needs the outer edge of the band at the level before it, and the start of bands laid out together needs the own start of each of them. Children bands laid out together may lie at different levels; conversion maps bands laid out together lie at one level, and are kept apart where their lines’ children bands lie on different sides of them (Conversion maps). So no start needs itself, and the starts of one side of a row are found step by step, until every band is laid out: each step lays out a band, with every band laid out together with it, once every band that lies between one of them and its line is laid out. Every order of the steps gives the same starts.

Level 0. A timeline’s line reaches out on each side as every line does (Segment lines, below): by its map stack (Conversion maps) and, when it is a segment line or draws the boundaries of chunks (section 8.10), by its boundaries, k both ways, and above by its segment labels, which belong to the line and to no band, so its segment labels lie beyond the maps against it. A drawn boundary spans k to either side of the line, so a line with a drawn boundary reaches at least k on both sides. A markers or conversion maps band at level 1 or −1 thus lies over the line’s boundaries, and every other band beyond them and its segment labels.

Conversion maps. The conversion maps band of a timeline holds its displayed maps (section 2.4) and those that its segments without a line join to it (Segments without a line). The present conversion maps bands of the lines of one row (Children) that lie on one side of those lines, at one level, are laid out together, also when there is only one, in one sequence of numbered slots that their maps share, except that those whose line’s children band on that side lies nearer the line are laid out together apart from the others, whose line’s children band lies further out or is not present there. Bands of one row on different sides, or at different levels of one side, are laid out apart. So no conversion maps band waits, through children bands, on its own start (Where a band lies, The order of the starts):

  • Their maps are taken in the traversal order of their lines (section 6.1), each line’s own maps in canonical order before those its segments join to it, segment after segment in traversal order, each segment’s in canonical order.

  • A map’s key is its name and its type when its name is not null, else its type alone. Keys are ordered by comparing Unicode code points: those with a name by name, then by type; those without after every key with a name, by type. An ID keys nothing: IDs are unique, so no two maps would ever share a slot.

  • The maps of each key are placed, in the order they are taken, by first fit (section 6.3) into that key’s sub-slots, each with the extent of the timeline it spans, from that timeline’s start to its end (sections 3.1 and 3.4). The sub-slots of the keys, the keys in key order, are the slots 1 to J. So maps of one key share a slot where their timelines do not conflict, and take the next where they do, whichever line of the row holds them; a band leaves empty every slot in which it holds no map.

  • The bands start at one distance c0 outwards from their timelines’ lines, the largest of their own starts (Where a band lies), so c0 = 0 at level 1 or −1. With w the largest stroke width of their lines, the map in slot j is centred c0 + w / 2 + j × (g + d) − d / 2 from the line of its band’s timeline, on the bands’ side, so that slot 1 lies g beyond c0 + w / 2 and each next slot g beyond the one before; and each of the bands is e = w / 2 + J × (g + d) wide, whichever slots its own maps take.

At level 1 or −1 a line’s conversion maps band is the line’s map stack on that side, which reaches e from the line; on every other side the line’s map stack is empty and reaches 0. A map spans its timeline, from Xs to Xe. A band places its maps, and section 7.2 writes them, in the order they are taken.

Which lines form a row is decided by Children: the root’s row holds the root alone, and every other row is one that the first fits of children bands laid out together fill. The slots depend on nothing else of the layout: only on which lines form the row, the displayed maps their bands hold, the extents of the timelines those maps span, and the side and level of each band and on which side of it its line’s children band lies.

Segment lines. Every segment of a segment line except its first (in canonical order) has a boundary: a thin vertical rule at x of the segment’s start, k to either side of the segment line’s line. A line’s boundaries are those of its own segments, drawn on it; the boundary of a segment that is itself a segment line lies on its segment line’s line, not on its own. The labels (section 5.3) of the segments of one segment line, in canonical order, are placed by one first fit into segment-label tiers above its line, each centred on x of the midpoint (section 3.1) of its segment’s start and end (the end of section 3.4, so a segment of unknown length ends where the next segment starts, and the last where its segment line ends). Tier n has its baseline at y − (m + 2 + (n − 1) × h(9)) − d(9), y the line’s ordinate and m the largest of the reach e of the line’s stack above, the width of its markers band when that band lies at level 1, and, when the line has a drawn boundary, k, so that the segment labels lie beyond the band against the line above, whichever of the two it is. A segment that is not a segment line has no row, no name in the name column, and no element type: a selector naming element never matches it, only its boundary or label. A line therefore reaches above m plus 2 + T × h(9) when its labels fill T ≥ 1 tiers, and m when they fill none; and below the larger of its stack’s reach below and, when it has a drawn boundary, k. “Above” is up whichever side of its parent’s line the line lies on, as for every band.

Segments without a line. A segment that is not a segment line has no line of its own, and its bands lie at its segment line’s line: what each of its displayed bands holds joins its segment line’s band of the same facet, after the segment line’s own content, segment after segment in traversal order, and that band draws it and lays it out as it lays out its own. So the segment’s maps lie in its segment line’s conversion maps band, each spanning the segment and taking a slot by its key and extent as every map of that band does (Conversion maps), not after the segment line’s own maps, so that the maps of one key on two segments may share a slot; its children in the children band, its regions in the regions band, and its named timestamps’ wedges and labels in the markers band, each at the segment’s start plus its coordinate (section 3.1); its own band order places nothing. Its named timestamps’ cross-section lines, which it holds itself (section 2.1), are drawn as every cross-section line, across the card, their gaps where their wedges and labels stand in its segment line’s markers band (section 6.6). Section 2.4 states when a segment adds nothing. A band of a segment line is present when it draws anything, of its own or of its segments, also when its own display is none, since each segment’s bands are displayed or hidden on their own (section 2.4).

Markers. The markers band of a timeline draws its named timestamps, in canonical order (section 2), and after them those that its segments without a line join to it, segment after segment in traversal order, each segment’s in canonical order (Segments without a line): of each timestamp its wedge and its labels (section 5.3), those that are displayed (section 2.4), at X, x of its position (section 3.1). After them it draws the secondary coordinates (section 2) on its timeline and on those segments, of every named timestamp whose cross-section lines cross its line in the card, in the canonical order of their timestamps, each by its wedge and its labels at X, x of the line, as a timestamp of the band is drawn; the defaults hide them (section 4), and one hidden takes no room. A timestamp’s displayed labels form its stack, one label per line in the order name, ID, coordinate, the first nearest the wedges; a timestamp without a displayed label has no stack. The stacks are placed by one first fit (section 6.3), in the order of their timestamps, into tiers: each stack is one item, as many lines high as it has labels, whose extent is that of one label as wide as its widest label, centred on X. A tier is as many lines high as its highest stack, and each of its stacks starts on its first line. Counting lines outwards from the wedges’ room (section 6.6), from 1, the label j of a stack in tier k lies on line n = m1 + … + m(k−1) + j, mi the height of tier i in lines. The band is w / 2 + 13 wide when it draws no label, or w / 2 + 13 + 2 + T × h(9) (left to right) when its tiers hold T ≥ 1 lines together, w the stroke width of its timeline’s line, T the line count here; section 6.6 places the wedges and the labels from the band’s start. This is each stack’s unturned place, where it lies when its timestamp’s wedge has the rotation 0: at the thick end of the wedge, beyond its base. The room of the wedges lies between the line and the labels whether or not a wedge is displayed, so that showing or hiding a wedge moves no label; a band whose timestamps draw labels and no wedge is as wide as with them. A turned wedge carries its stack round with it, from its unturned place to the thick end of the turned wedge (section 6.6, Labels of named timestamps); the tiers, the first fit and the band’s width are those of the unturned places, so that turning a wedge moves no other label and takes no room in its band.

After the secondary coordinates the band takes the room of the label stacks of elisions on its timeline: of every gap the card draws for an elision lying on the timeline (section 8.9) whose display lists a label, a card elision’s and a derived one’s in the card that draws the gap, a group elision’s in the card of its own timeline in each frame that draws its gap, never a gap carried into an ancestor card, in the order of the gaps from the left. Each gap has one stack, its labels in the order name, ID, coordinate, the first at the top, the words of the elision’s display, of a derived elision the card’s elision-display, choosing them. A stack enters the band’s first fit after the stacks of the named timestamps’ coordinates, primary and secondary, as their stacks do, one item as many lines high as the lines it reaches in its drawn tier (section 6.6, Elisions): m1 + … + m(k − 1) + j for a stack of j labels in drawn tier k, mq the height of drawn tier q in lines, so j in drawn tier 1. Its extent is that of one label as wide as its widest label centred on L × um, um the proportion (section 3.2) of the midpoint (section 3.1) of its gap’s piece on the root’s axis (section 8.9), and for a group elision (p + q) / 2 of its piece in the frame, in binary64: its place at full scale, so that its tier in the band does not depend on the widths of the gaps; its height does, through its drawn tier. It lies at its unturned place beyond the room of the wedges, which an elision leaves empty. The band takes the stack’s room there, so the stack’s room sets the band and the card’s rows, but the stack is drawn centred below its gap’s ellipsis, in its drawn tier (section 6.6), whether or not the ellipsis is drawn, so that showing or hiding an ellipsis moves no label. A band holding a displayed elision label is present, and its width counts the tiers as for a timestamp’s labels.

A child’s present markers band is one of its bands like any other: it takes a level, widens its side and so its edge, and its row takes that room (Children). The band never holds a cross-section line: a named timestamp’s cross-section line is held by its timeline’s line (section 2.1), is drawn when it is displayed, whether or not its wedge, its labels or this band are, and takes no room in any band (section 6.6).

Children. The children band of a timeline places its displayed children that have a line of their own, every child that is not a segment and every segment that is a segment line, in canonical order, by first fit. Each is placed as a whole: its line together with everything its own bands draw, at any depth, laid out from its line by this section.

A child has two extents, each found from its own subtree alone, from the deepest timelines up, before any first fit of its parent’s band:

  • its line extent, the smallest interval that holds the extent of its line and the line extents of its own children that have a line of their own, at any depth: what its lines alone reach;

  • its whole extent, the smallest interval that holds its line extent and the extent of every item that a first fit of its own drawing places at any depth (its segment labels, the stacks of named timestamps’ labels, the regions and region labels of its bands, each at its own extent whichever tier it lies in, and the whole extents of its own children; a child draws no flow control, section 6.4, Gathering), a stack where its wedge carries it, its extent’s centre moved by its carry vx (section 6.6, Labels of named timestamps): [(L × u + vx) − wi / 2 − 1, (L × u + vx) + wi / 2 + 1], and the extent [L × u + a, L × u + b] of every wedge it draws, u the proportion of the position it marks and a and b the smallest and the largest of 0, 13 × s − 3 × k and 13 × s + 3 × k, the offsets of its points from X (section 6.6, Wedges), so [L × u − 3, L × u + 3] for a wedge not rotated. These items are those of the child’s drawing laid out alone, the child the one line of its row and its descendants’ bands laid out together only among themselves (Rows of one band order, below), so that a turned wedge carries its stack from the tier that layout gives it (section 6.6), whatever tier the drawing as a whole gives it.

The cross-section lines a child draws add nothing to either extent: each lies at a position of its timeline and takes no room (section 6.6). Nor do the maps it draws, at any depth: each spans a timeline that lies within the line it is drawn at, so no first fit of this paragraph depends on a map’s slot. Neither extent depends on the row, the level or the tier of anything outside the child’s subtree, so both are known before the first fit that places the child. Children bands laid out together (below) are aligned when every child they place has one band order (The band order). The lines of a row are those that the first fits of their parents’ children bands, laid out together, place in it, and the root’s row holds the root alone; so which bands are laid out together, and whether they are aligned, is known row by row from the root outwards, each time from the band orders of children whose parents’ rows are already known. Aligned bands place each child by its line extent: a child keeps its row whatever the width of its labels, which the rows keep apart by their label tiers instead (Rows of one band order, below). Bands that are not aligned place each child by its whole extent. The extent of a line runs from its start to its end; when its end is open (section 3.4), it runs on to L × u + 9.5w, u the proportion of its end and w its stroke width, so that its three chevrons (section 6.6) take room in its row: 9.5w is the distance from the end to the outer chevron’s tip. This end of the extent is summed as written, L × u plus the constant 9.5w (section 3.3), measured from the root’s start like every extent and only compared, never computed from Xr; the drawn tip, and the right edge of section 6.7, is Xr + 5.5w. So a child placed by bands that are not aligned takes the room of its whole drawing, also where that reaches beyond its span, as a label wider than the child does, and every child the room of its children, as the content of a segment of unknown length that runs on past the next segment’s start (section 3.4). Since an item that starts where another ends shares its row (section 6.3), the segment lines among one segment line’s segments lie in one row when nothing of theirs reaches beyond their spans.

A child’s edge on a side is the outer edge of that side of its own drawing (Where a band lies). The lines of a row lie at one ordinate: the root’s row holds the root, and every other row is a row of children bands laid out together. The present children bands of the lines of one row that lie on one side of those lines, above or below, are laid out together, also when there is only one:

  • They start at one distance c0 outwards from their timelines’ lines, the largest of their own starts (Where a band lies).

  • Each places its own children by its own first fit, and their rows are numbered together: row k holds every child that any of them places in its row k, and lies at one distance lk outwards from their timelines’ lines.

  • Row k has an inner half (towards their timelines’ lines) and an outer half: each is p / 2, p their row pitch (section 6.2), or the largest edge of row k’s children on that half’s side, whichever band places them, when it is larger. The outer half’s side is the bands’ own side and the inner half’s the opposite one, so a child in a band above its parent’s line has its own above side in its row’s outer half.

  • With ik and ok the inner and outer halves of row k, row 1 lies at l1 = c0 + i1 and row k + 1 at l(k+1) = lk + (ok + i(k+1)). A band whose children fill C rows is i1 + o1 + i2 + o2 + … + iC + oC wide, summed left to right: its own rows, with the halves they have together.

A child in row k has its line at yl − lk, yl its parent’s, when its band lies above its parent’s line and at yl + lk when it lies below, and its own bands lie outwards from its line. The lines of a row lay out their own children bands together in turn, at every depth, so the sections of a row of movements share their rows whichever movement holds them.

Rows of one band order. The lines of a row placed by aligned children bands have one band order and lay out their bands together, facet by facet, so that a label of one line reaching over the span of another lies among the labels of its own facet alone, which one first fit over the row keeps apart:

  • On each side, the band of each of the five facets is present for every line of the row when it is present for one of them (Levels), so that the bands of one facet lie at one level; the band of a line that holds nothing of that facet draws nothing and takes the room of the others.

  • The bands of one facet at one level start at one distance c0 outwards from their lines, the largest of their own starts (Where a band lies), and each is as wide as the widest of them.

  • The markers bands place the stacks of all of them, line after line in the traversal order of the lines (section 6.1), each line’s in the order its own band takes them (Markers), by one first fit into one set of tiers.

  • The regions bands place the regions of all of them, line after line in that order, by one first fit into one set of rows, and the drawn labels of each row by one first fit into that row’s label tiers (Regions), so that Tk, prk and ak are the same for all of them; a label in tier j ≥ 2 is joined to its brace by a leader.

  • The segment labels of the row’s segment lines are placed, line after line in that order, by one first fit into one set of segment-label tiers, at one m, the largest of their lines’ m (Segment lines), so that every line of the row reaches as far above; a label in tier n ≥ 2 is joined to its segment by a leader (section 6.6, Leaders).

Their children bands and conversion maps bands are laid out together as every row’s are (above; Conversion maps). The root’s row holds the root alone, and the lines of a row placed by children bands that are not aligned lay out these bands each alone.

The layout takes three passes. First every first fit, which uses extents alone (section 6.3), so that which row holds which line, every row’s name size and pitch (section 6.2), and then every map’s slot and the count of slots of every conversion maps band (Conversion maps) and the first fits of the rows of one band order (above), which need the rows alone, are known before any distance is. Then the distances, from the deepest rows up: once every row of the children bands of a row’s lines has its halves, each of those lines has the starts and widths of its bands, in the order of the starts (Where a band lies), and so its edges, and the row its halves (the root’s row the outer edges of the root’s sides, section 6.5). Last the ordinates, from the root’s line (section 6.5) down.

No two children’s drawings overlap, and no two names of rows (section 6.6) do, but that two wedges may overlap where they mark positions closer than their widths, as two wedges of one timeline may, and that the stack of a turned wedge in a row of one band order, which the row’s first fit may put in another tier than its line laid out alone, is carried from that tier and may then lie elsewhere than the whole extent of an ancestor counts it (section 6.6): in a row of one band order the extents keep the lines and their children apart, and the first fits of the row the regions and the labels. The extents of two lines of a row never overlap, and meet at most where one ends and the other starts: one band places both by one first fit, or the bands of two lines of one row place them within those lines’ extents; and each row takes on each side the room that its children’s drawings take there. Rows laid out together lie at least a pitch apart, since every half is at least half the pitch, and the rows of the children bands of a row’s lines lie within that row’s room on their side (its half, or the root’s side) and at least half that row’s pitch beyond its ordinate; so any two rows lie at least half the sum of their pitches apart, and a row’s name is at most its row’s pitch high (section 6.2).

A child that is not displayed is not placed; section 2.4 states what it hides.

Regions. The regions band of a timeline places its displayed regions, in canonical order, by first fit with the extent from the smaller to the larger of their start and end, never widened by their labels, into R rows: a region keeps its row whatever its label’s width. On the line of a virtual timeline or the root line of a switched card, the chunks of its FlowMap come first, in the order of chunks, each an item whose extent is its drawn span, a gap among them (section 8.10), and then the regions. Then the drawn labels of each row, in the order of their regions, are placed by one first fit of that row, each with the extent of a label centred on Xm (section 6.6, Regions), into the row’s label tiers: tier 1 is where the label lies beside its brace, and tier j lies (j − 1) × h(9) further out. Row k, whose labels fill Tk tiers, or Tk = 1 when it has no drawn label, has the pitch prk = 6 + 2 + Tk × h(9) + S (section 6.2), so that a row without a label that needs a second tier is as high as it has always been. Row k has its brace’s arms at ak = r0 + (pr1 + … + pr(k − 1)) from the timeline’s line, the pitches summed first and a1 = r0, r0 the start of the band; the band is (pr1 + … + prR) − S wide, summed left to right. A label in tier j ≥ 2 is joined to its brace by a leader (section 6.6, Leaders). In a row of one band order the regions bands of the row’s lines take these first fits together (Rows of one band order).

Flow control. The root’s flow control band (Gathering) draws the band’s elements: the flow-control elements it draws, taken in the traversal order (section 6.1) of the tree they lie in, the tree of the top-level root, each timeline’s elements in canonical order; the elements of a timeline whose line or flow control band is not displayed draw nothing and take no room (section 2.4), and every other draws each of its parts that is displayed: a flow line its line and its stack, a jump its arcs, which the display of the element jump governs, its stack and its fork, and each its symbol. From its start c outwards it holds:

  1. the flow label tiers, T × h(9) wide when the stacks (Flow labels) fill T lines, and 0 wide when the band draws no stack;

  2. the flow row at f0 = c + T × h(9), where the flow lines end and the arcs of the jumps leave and arrive;

  3. the symbol row, from f0 to f0 + Hs (Symbols; Hs 0 when the band draws no symbol);

  4. the fork row, from f0 + Hs to f0 + Hs + Hf (Forks; Hf 0 when the band draws no fork);

  5. the jump rows of the arcs, beyond f1 = f0 + Hs + Hf, each sum taken left to right.

The band is T × h(9) + Hs + Hf + (4 + S) + (pj1 + … + pj(K − 1)) + 2 + TK × h(9) wide for K ≥ 1 jump rows, and T × h(9) + Hs + Hf wide for none, summed left to right as written, the pitches pjk of section 6.2 summed first; so a band drawing nothing but flow lines without labels is 0 wide and lies at the flow row of its start.

Flow labels. A flow line or a jump has a stack, the labels its cascaded label-display lists (section 2.4) of the three it can have (section 5.3), in the order name, ID, label, those whose text is null left out; the initial name label draws its name and its own label, so that naming an element shows its name. A displayed flow line, or a jump, whose stack holds a label draws it, a jump whatever its arcs show; a flow line that is not displayed draws none. The stacks, in the order of the band’s elements, are placed by one first fit into flow label tiers, each stack one item as many lines high as it has labels, with the extent of section 6.3, starting at x + 3, x that of its element’s position. A tier is as many lines high as its highest stack, and each of its stacks starts on its first line. Counting lines inwards from the flow row, from 1, the label j of a stack in tier k lies on line n = m1 + … + m(k − 1) + j, mi the height of tier i in lines, so the first label of a stack lies nearest the flow row: line n lies at the distance fn = f0 − (n − 1) × h(9) from the timeline’s line, and its label’s baseline at yl + fn − d(9) when the band lies below the line and at yl − fn + a(9) when it lies above it, yl the root’s line. T is the line count of the tiers together. The stack of a jump in tier k ≥ 2 is joined to the flow row by a leader (section 6.6, Leaders); a flow line needs none, since its line runs alongside its stack. Each displayed arc of a jump whose label-display lists label is labelled with its destination’s label (section 5.3).

Forks. The branches of a jump are its destinations whose manifestation is volta and whose place an arc would have (section 3.1), a destination with by 0 included: a destination whose to names nothing or an unread element is no branch. A jump with a branch draws one fork, where the fork is displayed, and no arc for a branch (Arcs): the fork shows that the flow leaves the jump on one of several ways, and its texts on which passes. The branches are taken in the order of their places, compared exactly, branches of one place in the canonical order of targets; of a fork of N branches, branch k, counted from 1, has the angle θk = min(35, 70 / (N − 1)) × (k − 1) degrees, 0 for N = 1, and the direction (ck, sk), the cosine and the sine of (θk × π) / 180 radians, each rounded to ten decimal places, ties to even, and read as the double nearest that decimal (as a wedge’s, section 6.6, Wedges), so that an engine whose sine errs by a few units in the last place draws every fork alike. So the fork row is as wide as the lowest tip of a drawn fork lies beyond its start, plus half a text height: Hf = (h(zf) / 2 + 18 × sM) + h(zf) / 2 (section 6.2), N the most branches a drawn fork has and sM the sk of the last branch of a fork of N branches (0 for one branch, 0.5735764364 for two, 0.9396926208 for three or more), so Hf = h(zf) when no drawn fork has more than one branch; room for every arrow and every text, the first text’s line starting at the row’s start. A fork is no label: it lies on the fork row at its jump’s x whatever its neighbours, so forks of jumps lying close may overlap, as symbols may; it is never placed by first fit and never moved, and its extent enters the extents of section 6.7 as a symbol’s does.

Arcs. Each jump whose arcs are displayed (its element jump) draws one arc per destination, from and to the positions of section 3.1, but none for a destination with by 0 (going on, no jump), none for a destination whose to names nothing or names an unread element (section 3.1), and none for a destination whose manifestation is volta, whatever the display of the element jump, since its jump’s fork shows it (Forks). The arcs, jump by jump in the order of the band’s elements and each jump’s in the canonical order of its targets, are sorted stably by span (the absolute exact difference of their to and from positions), smallest first, and placed by first fit with the extent from the smaller to the larger of their from and to, never widened by their labels, into K jump rows. Row k reaches its apex at the distance fk' = f1 + (4 + S) + (pj1 + … + pj(k − 1)) from the root’s line, the pitches summed first, 4 + S the first jump depth, so an arc that encloses another lies further out. Then the drawn labels of each row’s arcs, in the order of those arcs, are placed by one first fit of that row, each with the extent of a label centred on x of the midpoint of its from and to, into the row’s label tiers: tier 1 starts 2 beyond the apex, and tier j (j − 1) × h(9) further out. Row k, whose labels fill Tk tiers, or Tk = 1 when it has none, has the pitch pjk = 6 + S + Tk × h(9) (section 6.2). A label in tier j ≥ 2 is joined to its arc’s apex by a leader (section 6.6, Leaders). The band’s arc descriptors and labels follow this order of first fit (section 7.2).

A flow-control element that the reading in force does not read (section 1), a faded element, is drawn as any other and takes its room as any other, but with its arcs or its line, its symbol, its fork, their labels, its stack and their leaders at the opacity 0.35, which replaces the cascaded opacity and no rule changes. The reading in force also decides which arcs and branches a jump has and where a destination lands (section 3.1, Forks, Arcs): an unread section break is no section’s start, an unread jump target no place. Without a reading, these follow the rejection of the README (“Flow control”), an element some actor believes false.

Flow lines. A displayed flow line is a vertical line at x of its position, from the flow row across the root’s line and every band to the card margin V of the opposite side, inside its padding: from (x, yf), yf = yl + f0 below the root’s line and yl − f0 above it, to (x, P + V) when the flow control band lies below the root’s line and to (x, Hc − P − V) when it lies above it (section 6.5), ordinates of the member drawing counted from its top (section 8.4). A flow line of a descendant crosses every line and band in its way, as the root’s does.

6.5 Heights#

The root’s line lies at y_root = max(P + Ea, P + h(s) / 2) + V, Ea the outer edge of the root’s above side (section 6.4), its descendants’ drawings included in its children band, and V the card margin of the member drawing (section 8.6), drawn inside the padding above the first row or band. The content’s height is Hc = y_root + max(Eb, h(s) / 2) + V + P, Eb the outer edge of the below side, the card margin drawn again inside the padding below the last row or band. Where the member drawing has an ancestor band on a side (section 8.3), Ea or Eb is instead the outer edge of that band, which lies beyond every other band of that side. Here s is the root’s row’s name font size. So a card margin V adds V to the card above its contents and V below them, and moves its contents V down.

A root whose line is not displayed hides its whole drawing (section 2.4): none of its bands is present and its line reaches nothing, so the outer edges of both its sides are 0. Its row remains, with the name size s and the pitch p0 of section 6.2, which count its name although the name is hidden. The formulas above then hold with Ea and Eb the outer edges of its ancestor bands above and below (section 8.3), each 0 where it has none: y_root = max(P + Ea, P + h(s) / 2) + V and Hc = y_root + max(Eb, h(s) / 2) + V + P. Each of its ancestor bands starts at max(p0 / 2, S), the start of section 8.3 with the side’s outer edge and wm 0. Such a member drawing draws its displayed ancestor lines, with their displayed names and what they hold of named timestamps (section 8.3, Named timestamps), and nothing else, and lies in its card, Hm = Hc high, as every member drawing does (section 8.4). One without a displayed ancestor line draws nothing: y_root = P + h(s) / 2 + V and `Hc = y_root

  • h(s) / 2 + V + P`, and it gives section 8.2 no name, no centred label and no right edge.

6.6 Elements#

Every element below is drawn only when it is displayed, with the cascaded values of its element. Xs, Xe are x of an element’s start and end; y is the ordinate of its line (section 6.4); “outwards” points away from the line of the band’s timeline, up in a band above that line and down in one below it.

  • Root and children. A line with stroke width w from Xs; a filled circle of radius w centred at the start. The end tells what the timeline is; the first of these that applies draws it:

    • a timeline whose end is open (section 3.4) ends with three chevrons: the line runs on past Xe to Xr = Xe + 4w and stops there, and three chevrons pointing right follow it, each 1.5w long and 3w high, each 1.5w further right than the one before, so that the inner and the middle chevron each have their tip at the x of the next one’s arms:

      • the inner chevron through (Xr + w, y − 1.5w), (Xr + 2.5w, y) and (Xr + w, y + 1.5w);

      • the middle chevron through (Xr + 2.5w, y − 1.5w), (Xr + 4w, y) and (Xr + 2.5w, y + 1.5w);

      • the outer chevron through (Xr + 4w, y − 1.5w), (Xr + 5.5w, y) and (Xr + 4w, y + 1.5w), so its tip is at Xr + 5.5w.

      Each x is Xr plus its own constant multiple of w, each ordinate y minus or plus 1.5w, or y itself, and Xr is Xe plus 4w (section 3.3); no point is computed from another chevron’s. The line runs on 4w past its end and the glyph ends 9.5w past it, 12 and 28.5 with the default stroke width 3 of a timeline or child line; these are distances, and the tip is written, and is the right edge (section 6.7), as Xr + 5.5w. Relative to Xr, the inner and middle chevrons lie where an ancestor line that runs on to the right draws its two (section 8.3), and the outer chevron is what tells the marks apart: three chevrons say that a timeline’s length is unknown, two that an ancestor line runs on beyond the member it is clipped to. An ancestor line never ends in three chevrons, whatever its length or the member’s, and an open end never in two. The three chevrons stand for a root, a child, a segment line or a member alike, locked or not: an end that is not known has no position for a circle, an arrowhead or a diamond to mark;

    • a top-level timeline (a root of the manifest) whose end is known and whose locked is false ends with an arrowhead of length 3w and half-width 1.5w whose base is at Xe and whose tip is at Xe + 3w, the line ending at Xe, so that the drawn timeline is longer than its length; when it is a segment line, the arrowhead is open: the same triangle, stroked with width w / 2 and not filled, since prolonging it appends a segment;

    • a top-level timeline whose end is known and whose locked is true ends with a filled circle of radius w centred at Xe, like its start, the line ending at Xe;

    • a child timeline whose end is known, at any depth, ends with an open diamond of half-length 1.5w and half-width 1.2w centred at Xe and stroked with width w / 2, the line stopping at its left corner: the diamond marks where the child ends on its parent’s axis, and is the same whether the child’s locked is true or false. A member that is a child timeline ends so too, although its line is a timeline element.

    The circles and the arrowhead are filled and not stroked; the open arrowhead, the diamond and the chevrons are stroked and not filled, with no dash. Every arrow of a drawing follows one rule: its shaft ends at its head’s base, and the head continues the shaft’s direction at that point.

  • Boundaries. A line from (X, y − k) to (X, y + k), X the segment’s start and y its segment line’s ordinate (section 6.4), or X the drawn start of a chunk and y the ordinate of the line that draws it (section 8.10), drawn over the line.

  • Segment labels. Centred on x of the midpoint of the segment’s start and end, in its tier (section 6.4).

  • Wedges. A filled triangle, not stroked, its tip at X, at the distance c + w / 2 outwards from the line of its band’s timeline, c the start of the markers band (section 6.4) and w the stroke width of that line, so that with c = 0 the tip lies on the line’s edge. Its rotation r turns it about its tip: r is the angle in degrees from the outward direction to the direction from the tip to the middle of its base, the base lying to the left of X when r < 0 and to the right when r > 0, on either side of the line, so that a wedge turned −45 points at its coordinate from the lower left below the line and from the upper left above it. With s and k the sine and the cosine of r degrees, each rounded to four decimal places, ties to even, and read as the double nearest that decimal, its base’s corners lie at X + (13 × s − 3 × k) and X + (13 × s + 3 × k), at the distances c + w / 2 + (13 × k + 3 × s) and c + w / 2 + (13 × k − 3 × s) outwards, so that with r = 0 its base, 6 wide and centred on X, lies 13 further out than its tip. −180 and 180 draw the same wedge. The vertical through X meets a wedge turned by 13 to 167 in either direction at its tip alone; at 168 and beyond, up to 180, the base’s corners lie on either side of X and the vertical runs through the wedge’s body. A wedge takes the room of an unrotated one: its band is as wide whatever its rotation, and what of it lies beyond that room takes none; its labels go round with it (Labels of named timestamps). The wedges of a markers band mark its named timestamps’ primary coordinates and the secondary coordinates on its timeline (section 6.4, Markers; section 2): a wedge of a primary coordinate is turned by 0 unless a rule sets its rotation, and the defaults turn a wedge of a secondary coordinate −45 (section 4).

  • Labels of named timestamps. A timestamp’s stack lies at the thick end of its wedge, centred on the wedge’s axis, the line from the middle of its base to its tip, which points at the coordinate; whether or not the wedge is displayed, the stack lies where the wedge would carry it. At its unturned place (section 6.4, Markers), the wedge’s rotation being 0, each label is centred on X, x of its timestamp’s position, on its line of the band’s tiers: line n starts c + w / 2 + 13 + 2 + (n − 1) × h(9) out, c and w as for the wedges, the label gap included, and the label’s baseline lies beyond that start. A wedge turned by any other r carries its stack, kept upright, as one box: with s and k as for the wedges, j the number of the stack’s labels, W the width of its widest label, H = j × h(9) its height and D = 13 + 2 + (n1 − 1) × h(9) the distance from the tip at which its first line n1 starts at its unturned place, the box’s centre lies on the axis at the distance

    t = D + W / 2 × |s| + H / 2 × |k|

    from the tip, summed left to right, so that the box lies wholly beyond the line that crosses the axis at the distance D from the tip at a right angle, and touches it: beyond the middle of the base by the label gap 2, and by the lines of the tiers before its own. The stack’s carry is vx = t × s: its labels are centred on X + vx, and the box’s centre lies c + w / 2 + t × k out, summed whole first (section 3.3). Its lines, each h(9) high, fill the box from its side nearer the tip: when k ≥ 0, line i of the stack starts (c + w / 2 + t × k) − H / 2 + (i − 1) × h(9) out and its label’s baseline lies beyond that start, outwards; when k < 0, the box lying across the line, line i starts (c + w / 2 + t × k) + H / 2 − (i − 1) × h(9) out and its label’s baseline lies beyond that start inwards, away from the tip, as a label’s of a band on the other side of the line does. A wedge at the rotation 0 carries nothing (vx 0). Section 8.3 carries the stacks of the named timestamps drawn on an ancestor line so.

  • Cross-section lines. A vertical line at X, x of its named timestamp’s position (section 3.1), across the whole card of each member drawing that draws its timeline’s line, or draws its timeline as an ancestor line and the timestamp on it (section 8.3, Named timestamps), the ordinates being of the member drawing counted from its top (section 8.4). Let T be the timestamp’s timeline, whose line or ancestor line holds the cross-section line (sections 2.1 and 8.1), or, for a timestamp of a segment without a line, that segment’s segment line, whose line and bands are the segment’s (section 6.4, Segments without a line). The line runs from (X, yt) to (X, yb), yt the top of the card and yb its bottom, the card’s rectangle as sections 8.4 and 8.6 write it (an ancestor card’s as section 8.8 places it), its shadow not counted, whether or not the card is displayed: from the card’s upper border to its lower border, in every band order and whatever the card holds, less its gap, where the wedge and the labels of its primary coordinate stand on it. The gap spans these distances outwards from T’s line, c and w as for the wedges; on an ancestor line they are measured from it on its outer side, with c 0, w its stroke width and the tiers of its row (section 8.3):

    • when the wedge is displayed and the vertical through X runs through its body, that is when its rotation is −12 to 12 or beyond 167 in either direction (Wedges): the distance c + w / 2 of its tip and those of its two corners, c + w / 2 + (13 × k + 3 × s) and c + w / 2 + (13 × k − 3 × s);

    • when its stack holds a displayed label and lies across X, its labels being centred on X + vx and the widest W wide, so that X + vx − W / 2 ≤ X ≤ X + vx + W / 2 (Labels of named timestamps): the distances of the box’s two sides, (c + w / 2 + t × k) − H / 2 and (c + w / 2 + t × k) + H / 2; at its unturned place, with its stack in tier k' and n1 = m1 + … + m(k' − 1) + 1 its first line, the distance c + w / 2 + 13 + 2 + (n1 − 1) × h(9) at which that line starts and that distance plus j × h(9).

The groupstamp’s own wedge marks its primary coordinate and is turned by 0 unless a rule sets its rotation, as every named timestamp’s (Wedges). The positions of these lines are item coordinates of the frame, drawn at the frame’s place as every element of the frame is (section 8.6), and the lines take no room and widen nothing.

    Each of these lines, a faded one included, is a further drawing of the timestamp’s cross-section line (section 2.2): it carries the line’s address, belongs to T, has no settings of its own, and is drawn when the timestamp’s display lists cross_section and that line is displayed in M’s card of the frame, in the values cascaded for it there. It has no wedge and no labels, and nothing is drawn in a frame where the line holding the timestamp’s own cross-section line is not drawn. The timestamp’s wedge, its labels and its own cross-section line are drawn in every card as every named timestamp’s are.

    • d / 2, yc` the map’s centre (section 6.4), stroked and not filled.

  • Gap lines. Over the span of a gap of a FlowMap drawn on a line (section 8.10), from Xa to Xb, the line itself is not drawn; a gap line stands in for it, from (Xa, y) to (Xb, y), stroked in the grey #9CA3AF with the line’s opacity, its stroke width w and its stroke-dasharray, so that it keeps the line’s own stroke, solid on a continuous line and dotted (3 3 with the default width 3) on a discrete one, and differs from the line in colour alone; it has no mark at either end. Its colour is fixed: the line’s stroke does not reach it, and a rule restyling the line changes its width, pattern and opacity alone. It is part of its line, carries its address and has no element of its own.

  • Regions. A brace from Xs to Xe, arms at yb, tip 6 further out at Xm, x of the midpoint (section 3.1) of the region’s start and end, so it opens towards its timeline’s line; a chunk’s brace alike over its drawn span, and a gap of a FlowMap no brace, its label alone, placed as a brace’s label is; its label centred on Xm beyond the tip, 2 further out, in its label tier. The arms lie at the distance ak of their row (section 6.4), the tip at ak + 6, and the line of the label’s tier j starts at ak + 6 + 2 + (j − 1) × h(9), the label’s baseline beyond that start, each distance summed whole, left to right, before it becomes an ordinate (section 3.3), never computed from yb.

  • Jumps. Each arc of a jump (section 6.4, Flow control) is an arrow from (Xf, yf) to (Xt, yf), Xf = x(from), Xt = x(to) (section 3.1), yf the flow row, w the jump’s stroke width. Its curve is the cubic Bézier curve from P0 = (Xf, yf) to P3 = (Xt, yf) with controls C1 = (Xf + q, yc) and C2 = (Xt − q, yc), q = (Xt − Xf) / 4 and yc = yf + 4/3 × (ya − yf), ya the ordinate of its jump row’s apex: the controls lie a quarter of the span inwards, so the curve reaches its apex halfway, and a short arc arrives steeply and a long one flatly. The arrow is placed as TikZ places an arrow tip on a curve: the head points along the curve’s direction at its end, the unit vector u = (ux, uy) from C2 = (Cx, yc) to P3, ux = (Xt − Cx) / ℓ and uy = (yf − yc) / ℓ, ℓ = sqrt((Xt − Cx) × (Xt − Cx) + (yf − yc) × (yf − yc)) (the correctly rounded square root); the head is a filled triangle, not stroked, of length 4w and half-width 2w, its tip at P3 and its base centred at Bh = (Xt − 4w × ux, yf − 4w × uy), its corners Bh − 2w × n and Bh + 2w × n, n = (−uy, ux); the shaft is the curve with its end moved from P3 to Bh and its controls kept, so it meets the head’s base at a right angle.   • Symbols. A text centred on x of its element’s position, on the symbol row (section 6.4, Symbols), filled and not stroked.

  • Forks. Its stem and the shafts of its arrows, stroked and not filled, its arrowheads and its texts filled in the fork’s fill and not stroked (section 6.4, Forks).

  • Leaders. A leader joins a label or a stack moved out of its first tier to its element: a straight line at X, from the element’s point to the near edge of the label’s line, in the leader’s cascaded stroke (by default thin and light grey, section 4). For the label of a region in tier j ≥ 2 of row k, X = Xm, from the distance ak + 6 of the brace’s tip to ak + 6 + 2 + (j − 1) × h(9); for the label of an arc in tier j ≥ 2 of row k, X the x of the midpoint of its from and to, from the apex fk' to fk' + 2 + (j − 1) × h(9); for the stack of a jump in tier k ≥ 2, X the x of the jump’s position, from the flow row f0 to fn1 = f0 − (n1 − 1) × h(9), n1 the stack’s first line (section 6.4, Flow labels); and for the label of a segment, a child’s name on its segment line, in tier n ≥ 2 (section 6.4, Segment lines), X the x of the midpoint of the segment’s start and end, from the distance m above its segment line to m + 2 + (n − 1) × h(9), m as that section defines it (in a row of one band order the row’s, section 6.4, Children). Each distance is summed whole, left to right, before it becomes an ordinate (section 3.3). A leader takes no room and crosses whatever lies between its ends. A label in its first tier, or a flow line’s stack, has none. So one rule holds for the labels of regions, of segments, of arcs and of jumps, and for a child’s labels in a row of one band order: the element keeps its row or its place whatever its label’s width, and the label moves.

  • Names. For each row of lines (section 6.4, Children: the root’s row and every row of children bands laid out together, at any depth, so that all lines at one ordinate form one row), the names (section 5.3) of its drawn lines whose names are displayed, in traversal order, joined by , ; the line of a segment contributes no name, since its name is its label, so a row of segment lines alone has no name. When the content of the first of these names is not none, the row shows that text instead (an editor sets it to give a row its own name, and resets it to none). When the text is wider than that name’s max-width, it is cut to its longest prefix that, followed by … (U+2026), fits, and the cut text is shown. It is drawn starting at x = n (section 8.2), vertically centred on the row: baseline at y + (a(s) − d(s)) / 2, s the font size of that first name, whose address the text carries. Name descriptors are ordered from the top row down.

  • Elisions. A gap (section 8.9) over the stretch [a, b] of the card’s root axis, or over [p, q] of a frame’s proportions (section 3.2), is the rectangle from x(a) to x(b) (x(p) to x(q)), and from the top yt to the bottom yb of its card, the card’s rectangle as sections 8.4 and 8.8 write it, its shadow not counted; a gap across a frame (section 8.9, Group elisions) runs from the top of the topmost member card drawn in the frame to the bottom of the bottommost, as a groupstamp’s line does, over the cards and the gaps between them. It is filled with its fill and not stroked, and draws no rectangle while its fill is none; the rectangle is its extent all the same, in which an application finds it. A row crosses a gap when it is a line or a map drawn in that card, or in one of the frame’s member cards, the root’s, a child’s or an ancestor line alike, whose span holds the whole gap: its start (section 3.1) at or before a and its end (section 3.4) at or after b, compared exactly, or, across a frame, the proportions of its start and its end at or before p and at or after q, compared as doubles, the end of a member whose end is open (section 3.4, rule 2) counting as 1, and an ancestor line that runs on to the right (section 8.3) counting as open like it, so that it crosses every gap at or after its start; its ordinate is its line’s y, or a map’s centre yc. A row that crosses a gap stops at x(a) and resumes at x(b): its line or outline is drawn in pieces, from run to run, with no end mark where a piece stops at a gap and no start mark where one resumes after it, and nothing of it is drawn in the gap. A line or map whose start lies in a gap starts at that gap’s right edge, without a start circle, one whose end lies in a gap ends at that gap’s left edge, without an arrowhead, end circle, diamond or chevrons, and one whose start and end so meet or cross is not drawn. A line that really starts or ends at a gap’s edge keeps its mark there, since an edge lies in no gap. The pieces are written from the left, each a <line> of the line (for a map a closed outline over the piece), and then the line’s start and end marks (section 7.2); a piece that does not end right of where it starts, as where a line starts at a gap’s left edge, two gaps touch, or a child’s line ending at a gap’s right edge would stop at its diamond’s left corner before that edge, is not written.

    A gap draws, where its elision’s display lists ellipsis (for a derived elision the card’s elision-display), exactly one ellipsis, never one per row: three filled circles of radius 2 centred at xm − 6, xm and xm + 6, at the ordinate ym, with xm = (x(a) + x(b)) / 2 the gap’s middle and ym = (yt + yb) / 2 the middle of its height, yt and yb the top and the bottom border of the card that draws it, or, for a gap across a frame, the top of the topmost and the bottom of the bottommost member card (section 8.9), in binary64; an ellipsis is not drawn in a gap narrower than 20, x(b) − x(a) < 20. Where its display lists continuation, each row that crosses the gap gets a continuation, a line from (x(a), y) to (x(b), y), y its ordinate, drawn in the gap’s stroke, under the ellipsis. Its labels, the stack of section 6.4 (Markers), are drawn with the ellipsis, centred on xm below it, whether or not the ellipsis is drawn, in the stack’s drawn tier. The stacks drawn under one ym, those of one card’s gaps or those of one frame’s gaps across its member cards, are placed by one first fit (section 6.3) into drawn tiers, in the order of their gaps from the left, each stack one item as many lines high as it has labels, its extent that of one label as wide as its widest label, each label cut as below, centred on the gap’s middle as section 6.7 places an elision’s label, (l + r) / 2: so the stacks of neighbouring gaps that would touch or overlap as drawn lie one below the other, as the stacks of named timestamps do in their band (section 6.4, Markers). A drawn tier is as many lines high as its highest stack, and each of its stacks starts on its first line. The i-th label, from 1, of a stack in drawn tier k lies on line n = m1 + … + m(k − 1) + i, mq the height of drawn tier q in lines, and has its baseline at ym + (2 + 2 + (n − 1) × h(9)) + a(9), the dot radius and the label gap below ym, h(9) and a(9) those of the markers band that takes the stack’s room, in the card that draws them; that band takes the room of every line up to the stack’s last (section 6.4, Markers). Gaps never move apart for their labels: no gap is widened and no proportion changes. Each label is cut to its max-width as a name is cut (Names), never to the gap. Nothing else that lies on the stretch is drawn in a gap, but for the arc of a jump. An element that marks a position in a gap is not drawn: a wedge with its labels, a cross-section line, a groupstamp’s lines across a frame, a boundary, a segment label, a flow line with its stack, the stack of a jump, a symbol, a fork whose jump’s position lies in one, a region whose start, end or midpoint lies in one with its label, and an arc of a jump whose from, to or the midpoint of the two does with its label, each with its leader. A fork whose jump’s position lies in no gap is drawn whole at its elided x, of its one size, whatever gap it reaches. A region whose start, end and midpoint lie in no gap is drawn at their elided x, and its brace is cut at every gap it spans as a row is. Of the brace’s two straight parts at Ym, from X1 + k to Xm − k and from Xm + k to X2 − k (X1, X2, Xm, k and Ym as section 7.2 writes the brace), the part a gap meets leaves out the stretch from the larger of the gap’s left edge (x(a), or x(p) across a frame) and the part’s start to the smaller of its right edge (x(b), x(q)) and the part’s end, where that stretch has a length, with no tick or other mark at either end of the cut; cuts that touch are one cut, and the brace’s arms, its tip and its label are drawn whole. An arc whose from, to and their midpoint lie in no gap is drawn whole at their elided x, its arc across every gap it spans: the one element drawn in a gap that is not the gap’s own. A position at a gap’s edge lies in no gap, so a named timestamp there is drawn.

A label “beyond” a point at ordinate y lies outwards from it: below, its baseline is at y + a(9); above, at y − d(9), with the label gap added where stated.

6.7 Extents#

Section 8 places a member drawing in its card. Its names are the name texts it draws, its ancestor names included, and every centred label i it draws (segment and region labels and the labels of arcs, every label of a named timestamp’s stack on its own, the labels of its elisions, and its symbols, each centred on its element’s position, and its forks, each counted as a centred label of width 16 on its jump’s position, so that its stem reaches 8 left of it) has width wi and the proportion ui (section 3.2) of the exact position it is centred on, and its carry vi: a label of a named timestamp is centred at its timestamp’s position, moved by the carry its wedge gives it (section 6.6, Labels of named timestamps; section 8.3), a label of an elision at its gap’s middle (section 6.6, Elisions), for which L × u stands for (l + r) / 2, l and r the gap’s edges less x0: l = L × ua − D with D the shrinks of the gaps left of it, and r = L × ub − D' with D' those and its own shrink (section 3.2), so that no x0 enters it, and every other centred label has the carry 0. A centred label at X ends at (X + vi) + wi / 2. Its right edge is the largest right edge of any element it draws (lines, markers, wedges, braces, arcs, forks, leaders, symbols, labels and names). Right edges are geometric, no stroke width counted: a timeline’s start circle Xs + w, its arrowhead’s tip Xe + 3w, its end circle Xe + w or its diamond’s corner Xe + 1.5w, the outer chevron’s tip of an open end’s three chevrons Xr + 5.5w, with Xr = Xe + 4w computed first (section 6.6; 9.5w past Xe only as a distance, never as the sum Xe + 9.5w), the outer chevron’s tip of the two chevrons of an ancestor line that runs on to the right Xr + 4w, with Xr = x0 + L + 4w computed first (section 8.3; 8w past x0 + L only as a distance), a map’s Xe, a boundary’s or a cross-section line’s X, the largest x of a wedge’s three points (section 6.6), X + 3 for a wedge not rotated, the largest x of the points written in a brace’s or an arc’s descriptors (control points and an arc’s arrowhead corners included), the largest x of the points written in a fork’s descriptors (its arrowheads’ tips and corners included), a leader’s X, a gap’s x(b), the right end xm + 8 of an ellipsis, and a text’s end from its advance widths (a symbol’s and a fork’s texts included). In a card with gaps every x here is the elided one (section 3.2), and a line or map that ends in a gap ends at its left edge without its mark (section 6.6, Elisions).

7. Writers#

7.1 The page#

A writer draws one or more drawings, each placed at a canvas position (X, Y) with a scale z (one drawing alone: (0, 0) and 1). The page is the bounding box of the placed blocks, each W × z by H × z, plus a margin of 16 on every side: with left and top the smallest X and Y and right and bottom the largest X + W × z and Y + H × z, the page is PW = right − left + 32 wide and PH = bottom − top + 32 high, and a drawing at (X, Y) lies at the page position (X − left + 16, Y − top + 16), y downwards. Canvas units are CSS pixels in SVG, whose viewBox matches its unitless width and height, and points in TikZ.

7.2 Element descriptors#

A member drawing becomes a list of SVG element descriptors, in this order: the flow lines; the maps; the regions; the arcs of the jumps; the forks, each of these five band by band in the traversal order of the bands’ timelines, and each band’s in the order it places them (section 6.4: the flow lines and the forks in the order of the band’s elements); the root and then the children in traversal order; the boundaries, in the traversal order of their segments (a nested segment line’s boundaries thus follow its own boundary, not its siblings’); the wedges, band by band in the traversal order of the bands’ timelines, and each band’s in the order it places its timestamps and then the secondary coordinates on it (section 6.4, Markers); the names; the marker labels, the labels of the named timestamps’ coordinates in the same order, coordinate by coordinate, each one’s in the order of its stack, its name, its ID and its coordinate, whether or not its wedge is displayed; the leaders, in the order of the labels they join below; the segment labels, in the traversal order of their segments, the region labels, the labels of the arcs and the labels of the stacks, each stack’s in its order, name, ID, label, the stacks in the order of the band’s elements; the symbols, in the order of the band’s elements; and last the cross-section lines, in the order in which the markers bands place their timestamps, whether or not a wedge, a label or its band is displayed, so that they lie over everything else of the member drawing. Section 8.5 places them among the background, the titles, frames and cards, and the ancestor lines. Every descriptor carries the address of its element (section 2.2), the background the block’s empty address {}; the several descriptors of one element carry the same address. The SVG writer writes an address as up to three attributes, each only when the address has its member: data-id, the id; data-facet, the facet; and data-part, the words of part joined by one space (data-part="start label"). The background so carries none. An editor renders these same descriptors on screen; the SVG writer serializes them. An element’s hit area is the painted geometry of its descriptors, each standing for the element whose address it carries; this specification gives no element a hit area beyond them. So the three chevrons of an open end, part of their line like an arrowhead, carry the line’s address {"id": T} and hit the timeline.

A point in a template ({p1}, {P0}, {P3}, {Bh − 2w × n}, …) is written by where it stands. In a points attribute it is {x},{y}, its two numbers formatted by section 7.3 and joined by a comma without a space, and the points are separated by one space, as the polygon templates spell out; this holds for the chevrons’ polylines and an arc’s arrowhead too. 7.3 SVG profile#

8. The block: frames and cards#

Every manifest is an alignment bundle and is drawn as one block, whatever it holds: a manifest of one root as a block holding that root as an ungrouped root, a manifest of one group as a block holding that group’s frame, and every other manifest alike. The block opens with the manifest’s title (section 5.3), which an anonymous bundle, with neither a name nor an id, does not have. Every timeline drawn as a root (a member or an ungrouped root, a virtual timeline included, section 8.10) is drawn as sections 3 to 6 draw a root, in a card: an ungrouped root and a placed virtual timeline that is a member of no group in one card of its own, a member in one card in the frame of each group that has it as a member. The block’s items are its frames and the cards of its ungrouped roots and placed virtual timelines; each lies at its own place (section 8.6) and has its own graphical length L. The member drawings of one item share its L and one start x0, so that the members of a group by definition start and end at the same points. The shared end is x0 + L, found from x0 and L alone and never from a length: a member or ungrouped root of unknown length is drawn with its reach at the shared end (section 3.4), and its line runs on past it into its three chevrons (section 6.6).

8.1 What is drawn#

  • Groups. A block draws the manifest’s groups in canonical order (by ID), and then every ungrouped root, a root of timelines that no group names as a member and that contains no member, in canonical order (by ID). A root that contains a member is no ungrouped root: it is drawn as an ancestor line of that member and, unless a rule hides it, whole in an ancestor card behind the member’s frame (section 8.8). Then come the placed virtual timelines, those that no group names and whose ID places holds, in canonical order (by ID); a virtual timeline that no group names and places does not hold is not drawn (section 8.10). This is the block’s drawing order; an arrangement takes the items in its own order instead (section 8.6).

  • Frames. Each group is a frame, a frame element: a rounded rectangle holding the group’s title and then its members’ cards, a virtual timeline’s among them, one under the other, in the group’s display order: the entry of graphical.order under the group’s ID, or members when order has no entry for it. An ungrouped root has no frame.

  • Cards. A card is a card element, a raised rounded rectangle around one member drawing of a timeline M (in the tree below, a card holds nothing). An ungrouped root M, and a placed virtual timeline M, lies in one card; a member M lies in one card in the frame of each group that has it as a member, every card of M addressed {"id": M, "part": ["card"]} (section 2.2). A timeline that several groups have as a member is so drawn once in each of their frames, each drawing laid out with its own frame’s length and scale and its own card’s spacing, margin and size (section 8.6), and its elements carry the same addresses in every one. The card is the same whether it lies on the grey block or in a frame.

  • Member drawings. A member drawing is the drawing of sections 3 to 6 of one timeline M taken as the root: its line is a timeline element, its markers band draws its named timestamps, and positions are on M’s axis (section 3.1). A member that is a child timeline in timelines is still a timeline element in its member drawing, so rules selecting child do not reach its line, and it ends with the diamond, or with three chevrons when its length is null (section 6.6). Such a member’s drawing also has the faded lines of its ancestors, in an ancestor band on each side of its line where one of them lies (section 8.3): by default all of them below it.

  • Titles. A group’s title, a name element with the address {"id": G, "part": ["name"]}, opens its frame. The manifest’s title, a name element with the address {"part": ["name"]}, opens the block.

The tree of section 2.1: the block holds the manifest’s title, the ancestor cards and the timeline elements of their roots, the frames, and the cards and timeline elements of the ungrouped roots; a frame holds its title, its members’ cards and timeline elements and the gaps of its group elisions (section 8.9), each gap holding its ellipsis and its labels; a card or ancestor card holds nothing; a member’s timeline element holds what a root holds (section 2.1) and its ancestor lines; an ancestor line holds its name, the wedges of the named timestamps’ coordinates on it, primary and secondary, and their labels, each label beside its wedge and not held by it, and the cross-section lines of its timeline’s named timestamps drawn there (section 8.3). within names a timeline at any depth of timelines; a timeline’s depth (section 2.5) is its depth in its root’s tree, the roots being 1.

8.2 Horizontal#

The block has one horizontal layout, which every item applies at its own place: the name column, the line’s start and the right margin are found once over all items, so that items of one length are equally wide wherever they lie, unless a frame’s group elisions shorten it (below). Each quantity below is computed in item coordinates, measured from the item’s left edge, before the item’s scale (section 8.6); Li is the graphical length of item i.

The name column starts at n = Q + K when the block holds a frame and at n = K otherwise: inside the cards, at the same offset for every card of every item. Every row name of every member drawing, ancestor names included, is drawn at x = n. N is the width of the widest name of any member drawing of the block (section 6.7), and G' = 8 when any such name is drawn and 0 otherwise; titles are not names of rows and count for neither. The start is

s0 = max(n + N + G', max over i, j of (n + wj / 2 − (Li × uj + vj)), max over a of (n + N + G' + 8 × wa))

where j runs over every centred label of every member drawing of item i (section 6.7, with the member’s own proportions and the item’s length Li, and vj its carry, 0 unless its wedge is turned) and a over every ancestor line of the block that runs on to the left (section 8.3), wa its stroke width. So no label crosses the name column’s left edge, and no chevron crosses the gap after the names. Every member drawing of item i starts at s0, its shared end is s0 + Li, and the x0 of sections 3 and 8.3 is s0 in item coordinates.

A frame with group elisions is drawn shorter by DGi, the sum of the shrinks of its gaps (section 8.9), summed from the left, and 0 for every other item: its members’ shared end is s0 + (Li − DGi) and their drawn length Li − DGi, the L' of section 8.9. The right margin Mr is the largest of e − (s0 + (Li − DGi)) over every member drawing of every item i that draws anything, e its right edge (section 6.7); it may be negative. Item i’s right edge is Ri = max(s0 + (Li − DGi) + Mr, t), t the right edge of its group’s title, which starts at Q (none for an ungrouped root’s card), or Ri = max(s0, t) when no member drawing of the block draws anything; section 8.6 widens it on request. A block of one item so has the right edge of its widest content. A card inside a frame spans Q to Ri + K, so that every card of a frame ends at one x, unless its own request widens it (section 8.6); a frame spans 0 to Ri + K + Q; an ungrouped root’s card spans 0 to Ri + K + Q when the block holds a frame and to Ri + K otherwise, so that the frames and ungrouped cards of one length end at one x where no frame has group elisions, and a frame with group elisions ends DGi further left, unless its title holds it wider.

8.4 Vertical#

Every member drawing has its own height Hm, its content height Hc of section 6.5 with its ancestor bands included in Ea and Eb and its card margin V above and below; its card is Hm high, and the drawing’s top is the card’s top. The card padding K is horizontal only: above and below, the member drawing’s own padding P (section 6.5) lies inside the card. A card’s shadow lies 2 to the right of and 2 below the card.

A frame or card spanning x = a to b and y = c to d is written with x a, y c, width b − a and height d − c, its right and bottom edges being b and d: a card’s b is the end of its span (section 8.2) and its d its top plus Hm, a frame’s d its top plus its height below. A card’s shadow is written at x + 2 and y + 2 with the card’s width and height, and its right and bottom edges are b + 2 and d + 2.

  • A frame is laid out from its top: the title’s baseline lies at top + Q + a(s), s the title’s font size, so the title sits at the top of a slot h(s) high, unlike a row name, which is centred on its row. The frame’s margin Vf (section 8.6) lies after the title’s gap: the first card starts at top + Q + h(s) + 4 + Vf (at top + Q + Vf when the title is not displayed), and each further one the frame’s spacing Sf below the previous one’s bottom (section 8.6); the margin lies again after the last card, before the bottom padding Q. The frame is Q + h(s) + 4 + Vf + Hm1 + Sf + Hm2 + … + Sf + Hmn + Vf + Q high, summed in that order, without h(s) + 4 when the title is not displayed, and without either Vf when it holds no card.

  • The block. The manifest’s title, when there is one, lies at the block’s top left corner: drawn at x = Xl + P, with its baseline at Yt + P + a(s), both in place coordinates (below), so in the block at P and P + a(s) wherever the block grows. Its slot, h(s) high, is the top border of the items: no item rises into it, and an item placed higher pushes it up with the block’s top. Every frame and ungrouped card lies at its place (section 8.6).

Within a member drawing, the root’s line lies at the drawing’s top plus the y_root of section 6.5, computed in that order; every other ordinate follows from that line as section 3.3 states.

The block grows in every direction to hold its content, with the padding P on every side. Every position is first computed in place coordinates, measured from the place origin, as sections 6 and 8 write it (an item’s positions through its item coordinates and its place, section 8.6). In place coordinates the content runs from Xl, the smaller of 0 and the smallest left edge of any frame, card or displayed ancestor card (section 8.8) minus P, to Xr, P plus the largest right edge of any of these, a shadow included, or of the manifest’s displayed title, whose right edge is Xl + P plus its width; and from Yt, the smaller of 0 and the smallest top of any of these minus Pt, to Yb, P plus the larger of the largest bottom edge of any of these, a shadow included, plus the block’s margin Vb (section 8.6), and of the manifest’s displayed title, whose slot ends at Yt + P + h(s) (Xr = 2P + K and Yb = P when the block holds none of these). The content is Wb = Xr − Xl wide and Hb = Yb − Yt high, the sum with P taken first: Wb = (P + max(b + 2, …)) − Xl, and Hb likewise. The place origin lies at (Ox, Oy) = (0 − Xl, 0 − Yt) in the block, so an origin of 0 is +0; it is (0, 0) unless an item reaches left of or above the padding. The block is W = max(Wb, width) wide and H = max(Hb, height) high (W = Wb when width is null, H = Hb when height is); the background is a rectangle filling it with corner radius rx.

8.5 Descriptors#

A manifest becomes element descriptors in this order: the background; the manifest’s title; then each displayed ancestor card in the order of section 8.8, the descriptors of its member drawing and its gaps; then, for each frame in drawing order, the frame, its title, and for each of its members in display order, the member’s card and the descriptors of its member drawing; then, for each ungrouped root and each placed virtual timeline in drawing order, its card, the descriptors of its member drawing and its gaps. A card’s gaps (section 8.9), its own and those carried into it, follow its member drawing, gap by gap from the left, each gap’s descriptors in this order: its rectangle where it is filled, its continuations, its ellipsis, then its labels, name, ID and coordinate. A gap’s rows, which its continuations follow, are taken in their own order, which is not that of their descriptors (section 7.2, maps before lines): first the ancestor lines, from A1 to Ak (section 8.3), then the lines of the member drawing in the traversal order of their timelines (section 6.1), each line followed by the maps of its conversion maps band in the order that band places them (section 6.4); a row is taken only where it is drawn and crosses the gap (section 6.6, Elisions). The descriptors of a member drawing follow section 7.2, with its ancestor lines, from A1 to Ak, each with its chevrons, just before its root; the wedges of the named timestamps’ coordinates on them, from A1 to Ak, each ancestor’s in the order of its first fit (section 8.3), first among its wedges; their names, from A1 to Ak, first among its names; the labels of those coordinates, from A1 to Ak, each ancestor’s in the same order, first among its marker labels; and the cross-section lines of their timelines’ named timestamps, from A1 to Ak, each ancestor’s in canonical order, first among its cross-section lines. After the last item come the gaps of the frames’ group elisions, frame by frame in drawing order and in each frame from the left, each written as a card’s gap is, its rows taken card by card from the top; then the groupstamps’ lines, timestamp by timestamp in the order of timestamps and frame by frame in the order of groups, each frame’s lines from the top down (section 6.6); then the alignment anchors and then their labels (section 8.7), so that they lie over everything else. In an arranged block (section 8.6) the item order, or for horizontal the row, takes the place of drawing order throughout: the frames and the ungrouped roots’ cards are written in that one order, a frame or a card each where it stands in it, and the gaps of the frames follow it too. In a block arranged horizontal the ancestor cards written are its once cards (section 8.8, Drawn once), in the order of their roots from the top, each followed by its gaps from the left as a card’s gaps are, a seam writing nothing; a line of the member drawing cut by gaps is written as its pieces from the left, in the line’s place. A title is a text descriptor of section 7.2, anchored at start; its content and max-width apply as section 6.6 applies them to a row’s name.

8.6 Places#

Every item of the block, a group’s frame or the card of an ungrouped root or of a placed virtual timeline, lies at a place: its left edge x and its top y, measured from the place origin in canvas units, its graphical length length, its scale scale (positive), its spacing (finite, ≥ 0), its margin (finite, ≥ 0), and its requested size width and height (positive, or left out for its content’s own size). places (section 1) states any of these members for the item whose ID is its key, and a group’s entry states in its cards, under a member’s ID M, the card of M in that group’s frame, places[G].cards[M]. A key naming no item, such as a member’s ID at the top of places, or a key of cards that is not a member of its group, is not used, and so is any of a value’s members that this section gives no use for (a member’s card uses only width, height, spacing and margin, and an ungrouped root’s card no cards). A member that is not stated takes its default:

  • length: the placement’s length;

  • scale: 1;

  • spacing: 8 for a frame, 4 for a card;

  • margin: 0;

  • width and height: none, the content’s own size;

  • x: P;

  • y: for the first item in drawing order (section 8.5), P + h(s) + F + Vb below the manifest’s title, s its font size and Vb the block’s margin, or P + Vb when there is no title or it is not displayed; for every further item, the block’s spacing below the largest bottom edge d of the items before it and of their displayed ancestor cards (section 8.8), and lower still where its own ancestor cards rise above it (section 8.8, No room). That edge is the frame’s or card’s d of section 8.4, its place y plus scale times its height, summed in place coordinates before any shift; a card’s shadow does not count, and an item that a height request makes higher ends at its requested bottom.

So with places empty the items lie one under the other, as an editor first shows them.

Arrangements. An arrangement (section 1) takes the block’s items in its own item order: the items its order names, each at its first mention, an ID that names no item passed over, and then every item it leaves out, in drawing order (section 8.1). An arranged item takes no x and no y from places, which keeps them for when the block is not arranged; every other member of its place applies as above.

  • vertical: every item lies at its default x and y, as above, the items taken in the item order instead of drawing order: the first at the first item’s default y, each further one the block’s spacing below the largest bottom edge of the items before it and of their displayed ancestor cards, as above.

  • horizontal: the items stand in one row, in the row of section 8.8 (Drawn once), which keeps the item order wherever the stretches of the groups on their roots leave it free. The first item lies at x = P, each further one at the previous item’s x plus its width plus the row gap max(S, 48), S the block’s spacing, summed in that order; an item’s width is its drawn width, scale times its b − a (section 8.4), a card’s shadow not counted. Their tops, and the once cards of the roots they hang on, are those of section 8.8; where no group hangs on a root every item’s top is the first item’s default y. When the rules of section 8.8 refuse the row, the block is drawn as vertical, in the item order, and the drawing states why.

An item is laid out by sections 8.2 and 8.4 with its own length as L, in item coordinates, its left edge and top at 0, every sum written as those sections write it. Its frame or card is wider when width asks for more: for a frame, Ri is taken as the larger of its Ri and width − K − Q, so its cards still end at one x inside it; for an ungrouped root’s card, as the larger of its Ri and width − K − Q when the block holds a frame and width − K otherwise. It is higher when height asks for more: the frame or card is the larger of its own height and height high, every card and member drawing keeping its top. width and height are in item coordinates, before the scale.

The card spacing S of a member drawing (sections 6.2 to 6.5 and 8.3) is its card’s spacing: the entry places[G].cards[M] for a member M drawn in the frame of group G (section 1), or the entry under the ungrouped root’s ID. A group’s entry states its frame’s spacing Sf, the gap between its member cards (section 8.4); the placement’s spacing is the gap between the block’s items at their default y. The card margin V of a member drawing (section 6.5) is its card’s margin, from the same entry; a group’s entry states its frame’s margin Vf, and the placement’s margin is the block’s margin Vb (section 8.4). A container’s margin is drawn inside its padding, above its contents and below them (section 1), and changes no width. Each container’s spacing and margin space only its own contents: none multiplies another, and a frame’s padding Q, its title’s slot, the block’s padding P and the card’s padding P above and below do not change with any of them.

A member’s card may request a size, a spacing and a margin of its own: the entry places[G].cards[M], for the card of member M in the frame of group G, states its width and height, in item coordinates, before G’s scale, its spacing and its margin, and nothing else of it is used; the cards of M in other groups’ frames take their own entries. The card is then the larger of its own width and width wide, spanning Q to max(Ri + K, Q + width), and the larger of Hm and height high; its member drawing keeps its place at the card’s top left, so the request adds room to its right and below. The frame holding it grows with it: the frame spans 0 to the largest right edge of its cards plus Q, and its height sums its cards’ heights.

The item is then drawn at its place: every position of its elements, its frame’s or card’s included, is the item-coordinate position (px, py) taken to (x + scale × px, y + scale × py), and every length of them, a width or height, a radius (a frame’s or card’s corner radius rx included), a stroke width, every dash of a dash array, a font size, a text’s max-width and the size of every marker, is multiplied by scale; a frame’s or card’s width and height are scale times its item-coordinate b − a and d − c; a card’s shadow is written at its card’s written x and y plus scale × 2, with the card’s width and height, its right and bottom edges for the block being x + scale × (b + 2) and y + scale × (d + 2); with scale 1 that is (x + px, y + py) and every length unchanged. So an item’s scale scales everything in it, proportions kept, text included, about its top left corner, as the block’s scale z scales the block (section 1). Its edges for the block (section 8.4) and for the default y above are its drawn edges, after the scale.

The members of a group are never placed: they lie one under the other in their frame, in the group’s display order (section 8.1), and only that order changes; their cards may only request a size, a spacing and a margin. The bands of every timeline likewise lie outwards from its own line in its band order (section 6.4).

8.7 Alignment anchors#

Every alignment anchor of a named MatchStamp (section 2) is placed between the cross-section lines of its two timestamps, in the block’s coordinates, after every item is drawn at its place (section 8.6) with Ox and Oy added (section 8.4). A timestamp has one cross-section segment in every member drawing in a frame or card, an ancestor card’s excepted, that draws its timeline’s line, or for a segment without a line its segment line’s line, or draws its timeline as an ancestor line and the timestamp on it (section 8.3): the vertical segment from (X, yt) to (X, yb) of section 6.6 as drawn in the block, from the card’s upper border to its lower border, its gap included. A segment counts whether or not its cross-section line is displayed. An anchor one of whose timestamps has no cross-section segment draws nothing.

Between a segment A from (Xa, a1) to (Xa, a2) and a segment B from (Xb, b1) to (Xb, b2), a1 ≤ a2 and b1 ≤ b2, the line is the shortest one, with lo = max(a1, b1) and hi = min(a2, b2):

  • when lo ≤ hi, the two overlap vertically, as on cards side by side, and the line is horizontal at their middle ym = (lo + hi) / 2, from (Xa, ym) to (Xb, ym);

  • when a2 < b1, A lies above B, and the line runs from (Xa, a2) to (Xb, b1);

  • otherwise from (Xa, a1) to (Xb, b2).

A is a segment of the first timestamp of the anchor’s timestamps and B of the second. When a timestamp has several segments, its timeline being drawn in several cards, the line is the shortest over every pair of one segment of each: the one whose dx × dx + dy × dy is smallest, dx and dy the differences of its end points’ coordinates, in binary64; of equal ones, the first in the descriptor order of the first timestamp’s segments, then of the second’s (section 8.5). A line of no length is not drawn. The line is straight, crosses whatever cards lie between its ends, takes no room and widens nothing.

The alignment_anchor element, with the address {"id": A} of its anchor’s ID A, is the <line> of section 7.2 from the first point to the second. When its anchor’s display lists name, the MatchStamp’s name is drawn as a label element with the address {"id": A, "part": ["name"]}, centred on the line’s midpoint (xm, ym), xm = (x1 + x2) / 2 and ym = (y1 + y2) / 2 in binary64, with its baseline at ym + (a(s) − d(s)) / 2, s its font size, vertically centred as a row’s name is (section 6.6); it is placed so whether or not the line is displayed or has a length, and it takes no room either.

The lines are written after the last item’s descriptors, MatchStamp by MatchStamp in the order of matchstamps and each MatchStamp’s anchors in the order of its alignment_anchors, and then their labels in the same order (section 8.5).

8.8 Ancestor cards#

A member that is a child timeline is drawn with the faded lines of its ancestors (section 8.3). Its root may also be drawn whole, behind the member’s frame, in an ancestor card: an ancestor_card element with the root’s card address {"id": R, "part": ["card"]} (section 2.2), belonging to the root R, drawn as a card is (section 7.2), with the member drawing of R in it. Every ancestor between R and the member is a row of that drawing at its true proportions, and R’s own line continues the member’s faded ancestor line of R beyond the frame’s borders. In the tree of section 2.1 the block holds the ancestor card and the timeline element of R drawn in it, and the ancestor card holds nothing, so nothing of its drawing lies inside an ancestor element and none of it is faded. The defaults show every ancestor card (section 4); a rule of a later layer hides them as it hides any element, all of them with

{"select": {"element": "ancestor_card"}, "set": {"display": "none"}}

and those of one root R with "addresses": [{"id": "#R", "part": ["card"]}] added to the selector.

Which. The groups are taken in drawing order (section 8.1), and the members of each group G in its display order. Each member M that is a child timeline gives one ancestor card of its root R (A1 of section 8.3) behind G’s frame, aligned to M, unless an earlier member of G lies below the same R: a root has at most one ancestor card behind a frame, aligned to the first member below it in display order, and a later member below it adds none, whether or not that card is drawn. A root with members below it in the frames of several groups has one ancestor card behind each of them, each laid out with its own frame’s length and drawn at its own frame’s place and scale; several roots with members in one frame have one ancestor card each. A root that is a member of another group keeps its card in that group’s frame too.

When it is drawn. An ancestor card is drawn when it is displayed (section 2.4), the line of R in its member drawing (below) is displayed, and the line it is aligned to is drawn in M’s card in G’s frame: R’s ancestor line there, or M’s own line where that ancestor line is not displayed. Otherwise nothing of it is drawn, it takes no room and the block does not grow for it.

Geometry. Let ts be the start of R on M’s axis (section 8.3, with Aj = A1 = R), λ M’s length, or its reach when its length is null, and λR R’s length, or its reach when its length is null (section 3.4); te = ts + λR, combined as section 3.1 combines values. us = ts / λ and ue = te / λ are proportions formed as section 3.2 forms them. With x0 the start of the member drawings of G’s frame and L its graphical length (section 8.2), in item coordinates and binary64:

xs = x0 + L × us
xe = x0 + L × ue
LA = xe − xs

  1. Alone. R is laid out by sections 3 to 6, 8.2 and 8.4 as the one ungrouped root of a block that holds nothing else and has no title: at the graphical length LA, with the card spacing 4 and the card margin 0 whatever places states, the name column at n = K, its gaps (section 8.9) on its elided axis (section 3.2), and its own N, G', start s0' and right margin, so that its card spans 0 to Ri' + K and is Hm high, its top at 0. R is a root, so its drawing has no ancestor band. yR is the ordinate of R’s line in it.

  2. Placed. yA is the ordinate, in G’s item coordinates, of R’s ancestor line in M’s card, or of M’s line where that ancestor line is not displayed. Every position (px, py) of the layout of 1, its card’s included, becomes (px + dx, py + dy) with dx = (xs + DL) − s0' and dy = yA − yR, DL the sum of the shrinks of the gaps left of M’s span (section 8.9), summed from the left, and 0 without gaps, less OL. The card’s axis has two seams (section 3.2), at the start −ts and at the end −ts + λ of M’s span on R: the first OL = x0 wide, the room of G’s frame from its left edge, at 0 in item coordinates, to its member drawings, and the second OR = Fw − (x0 + L') wide, the room from the members’ drawn end to the frame’s right edge, Fw the width of the frame as drawn and L' its members’ drawn length (section 8.9), in binary64. A position at either seam lies on the side of M’s span, so an element at the span’s start or end lies under the frame. So R’s line starts at xs + DL, M’s span on it lies over M’s line, its gaps carried from the frame (section 8.9) over the frame’s gaps, everything left of the span, the card’s names included, lies left of the frame, everything right of it lies right of the frame, OR further right and DG further left with the frame’s shared end, R’s line runs whole across both seams under the frame, and it lies at yA. The ancestor card is then drawn at G’s place and scale, as every element of the frame (section 8.6).

  3. No room. No quantity of the block’s one horizontal layout (section 8.2) and no size of G’s frame or cards counts it; but an item’s default y (section 8.6) counts the displayed ancestor cards behind the frames before it and behind its own frame: it lies the block’s spacing below the largest bottom edge of the earlier items and their ancestor cards, and so much lower that its own ancestor cards’ tops lie that spacing below too (the first item keeps its default y), shadows not counted. The block grows to hold it as it holds a card: its left edge, top, right and bottom edges, its shadow included, count among the edges of section 8.4, so the block may grow to the left or upwards, moving the place origin within it while every item keeps its place.

  4. Order. Ancestor cards are written before every frame and card, in the order of Which (section 8.5), so that every item and every alignment anchor lies over them, and a later ancestor card over an earlier one where they meet.

  5. Nothing else. places states nothing of it: a key places[G].cards[R] names no member of G and is not used (section 8.6). It gives no cross-section segment to an alignment anchor (section 8.7), and no groupstamp line is drawn for it, since those lines span the member cards of a frame alone (section 6.6, Cross-section lines); the named timestamps of its drawing draw their own cross-section lines in it, across the whole ancestor card, as in every member drawing.

The fixture group_parent_both drawn with the defaults shows the root piece (16 quarters) behind the frame of its child middle (8 quarters at offset 4), so us = −0.5 and ue = 1.5 and LA = 800 at the length 400; its proportion is 1 (section 8.9), so it derives no elision, and the card is written at x 8 and y 36.207, 1013.0391 wide and 63.3789 high, piece’s line runs from 60.3354 to 1004.0391 at y 85.1836, on the faded ancestor line of piece, which the default band order puts below middle’s line in middle’s card, and the block grows to the left, its place origin at (252.3354, 0). In the card middle’s row lies above piece’s line, at 51.207, 4 lower than middle’s own line at 47.207: between middle’s markers band and the line of piece, middle’s card holds the card spacing S and the inner half 7 of the row of piece, and piece’s drawing only the start 7 of its children band. In group_parents_mixed, three roots lie behind one frame, each aligned to the first of its members in the display order; sonata is drawn whole, session and page with the gaps they derive (section 8.9).

Drawn once. In a block arranged horizontal (section 8.6) every root that groups hang on is drawn once, behind the row of items, and each group stands where its stretch lies on that root.

Words. A group hangs on a root R when it has an ancestor card of R (Which, above): when one of its members lies below R, a child, a grandchild or deeper. The member that card is aligned to, the group’s first member below R in its display order, is the group’s anchor on R, and the anchor’s stretch runs on R’s axis from the anchor’s start (section 3.1) to its end (section 3.4), so that a member of unknown length stretches to its drawn end. A member that is the root itself anchors nothing; an ungrouped root’s card hangs on nothing.

The rules.

  1. The items stand side by side in one row, and each root that a group hangs on is drawn once behind them, the roots one under another.

  2. Along every root, the stretches of the groups hanging on it follow the row from left to right without overlapping; touching is allowed.

  3. In every group, the anchors lie in the same top-to-bottom order as their roots.

The orders. Rule 2 gives, for each root in canonical order (by ID) and each two groups hanging on it, taken in the item order (section 8.6), “G before H” when G’s stretch ends at or before the start of H’s, and “H before G” when H’s ends at or before the start of G’s, compared exactly: nothing when both hold (two stretches without length at one position), and an overlap when neither does. Rule 3 gives, for each group in canonical order and each two of its anchors next to each other in its display order, “the upper anchor’s root above the lower’s”. The row is the item order sorted by the first relation: repeatedly the first item of the item order left that no item left must precede. The roots, every root some group hangs on, are sorted likewise by the second relation, starting from canonical order. So the row keeps the item order wherever the stretches leave it free, and roots no group orders lie in canonical order.

Refusal. When no row and no order of the roots satisfy rules 2 and 3 for all groups at once, horizontal is refused, and the first of these names the conflict:

  • An overlap, on the first root in canonical order where two stretches overlap, of the first such pair in the item order: its groups are the pair, its root that root, and its reason "G" and "H" overlap on "R" (a–b), the stretch both cover, from the later start to the earlier end, written as an elision’s coordinate label writes its elided coordinates (section 5.3): "Theme A" and "Theme B" overlap on "Score" (8–16 ♩).

  • The row in a cycle: of the items the sort leaves, every item that must precede none of the others still left is set aside, again and again until none is. From the first item of the item order still left, step along the first relation, each time to the item earliest in the item order among those still left, until an item repeats. The cycle so found, begun at its item earliest in the item order, gives the groups, the root of each step (of two roots giving one step, the earlier in canonical order) and the reason, for two groups on "R" "G" comes first, on "S" "H", and for more on "R" "G" comes before "H", on "S" "H" before "J", on "T" "J" before "G".

  • The roots in a cycle, found and begun likewise among the roots in canonical order, every root that must lie above none of the others still left set aside first: the groups of its steps (of two groups giving one step, the earlier in canonical order), its roots and the reason "G" holds "R" above "S", "H" holds "S" above "R".

Each group and root is named in a reason by its title or name (section 5.3), in double quotes. A refused arrangement is drawn as vertical (section 8.6), and the drawing states the conflict (section 1); nothing else refuses one.

Vertically. In place coordinates, with S the block’s spacing: the once card of a root R is its ancestor card behind the first group of the row hanging on it, G1, aligned to G1’s anchor M1, laid out alone as step 1 above lays it out, at that card’s graphical length LA, and drawn at G1’s scale s1; yR is the ordinate of R’s line in that layout and Hm its height, so the card reaches aR = s1 × yR above its line and bR = s1 × (Hm − yR) below it. For the anchor M of a group G on R, yM is the ordinate in G’s item coordinates of R’s ancestor line in M’s card, laid out as section 8.4 lays out the frame, or of M’s line where that ancestor line is not displayed (the yA of step 2 above).

  1. Lines. The line of the first root lies at y1 = 0, and that of each further root R' at the largest of (y + bR) + S + aR', R the root above it and y its line, so that the cards of two roots never overlap, and, for each group whose anchor M' on R' comes after an anchor M on a root Q in its display order, M the one just before M', yQ + sG × (yM' − yM), yQ the line of Q and sG the group’s scale, so that the group can lie on both roots.

  2. Padding. In each such group the card of each anchor M' after the first lies δ = (yR' − yQ) / sG − (yM' − yM) lower than section 8.4 lays it, the gap above it grown by δ, yR' and yQ the lines of the two anchors’ roots: every card after it moves with it, no card changes its height, and the frame is the sum of its δ higher. So every anchor lies on its root’s line, up to the rounding of binary64.

  3. Tops. A group hanging on roots has its top at yR − sG × yM of its first anchor M, on the root R, and a once card at yR − aR. The row’s top T0 is the smallest of these tops; every item that hangs on nothing has its top at T0. Then every ordinate moves by Y − T0, Y the first item’s default y (section 8.6), so that the highest frame or once card lies where the first item would.

  4. Left. Where a once card’s left edge (below) lies left of P, the row is packed again from x = P + δx instead of P, δx that edge’s distance from P for the leftmost such edge, and every abscissa below follows from the new places.

The axis of a once card. R’s line in its once card is drawn in runs, each at the scale of one anchor’s frame. The run of an anchor M of a group G, at the place (xG, yG) with the scale sG, M starting at a on R’s axis and λ its length, or its reach when its length is null, gives a position t of R’s axis the x

XM(t) = xG + sG × (x0 + (L × u − D))

with u the proportion of t − a, M’s own position, of λ (section 3.2), x0 and L the start and the graphical length of G’s member drawings and D the sum of the shrinks of the gaps of G’s frame with q ≤ u (section 3.2): where R’s ancestor line in M’s card puts t, continued to either side of M’s span. Between two anchors next to each other along R, A, whose stretch ends at b, and B, whose stretch starts at a, with c the middle of the room right of A’s frame, the mean of that frame’s right edge and the left edge of the next item of the row, in binary64:

  • s is the largest whole unit of R’s axis (section 8.9, Derived elisions, step 1) no less than b with XA(s) ≤ c − 16, or b where none is, and e the smallest whole unit no more than a with XB(e) ≥ c + 16, or a where none is;

  • when s < e, a gap leaves out R from s to e: R lies on A’s run up to s and on B’s from e, and the gap is drawn from XA(s) to XB(e), at least the standard gap of 32 wide;

  • otherwise a seam (section 3.2) lies at m = max(b, min(a, s)): R lies on A’s run up to m and on B’s from m, the seam XB(m) − XA(m) wide. It leaves out nothing: it marks where the scale changes where the room holds no whole unit.

So every change of scale lies in a gap or at a seam. A gap is an elision the card derives on R (section 8.9), with the address {"id": R, "part": ["elision"]} (section 2.2) and the elided coordinates s and e, drawn as every gap is (section 6.6), its display the card’s elision-display. A seam is none, and a row crossing it is drawn across it whole (section 3.2). An element at m lies on B’s run when it belongs to a timeline that starts at m (its line’s start, its named timestamps there with their wedges, labels and cross-section lines) or is the start of a region at m or the place m of a jump’s destination, where its arc arrives, and on A’s run otherwise; a line ending at m ends there on A’s run, one starting there starts on B’s.

Left of the first anchor’s stretch R lies on that anchor’s run and right of the last one’s on the last anchor’s run, and there the card draws the gaps its elision mode gives (section 8.9): left of the first stretch those that R’s ancestor card behind the first anchor’s group draws left of its protected span, right of the last those that the one behind the last anchor’s group draws right of its own, with their natural and drawn widths taken from those cards and multiplied by their groups’ scales; the proportion r of their derived elisions is the least of the proportions of R’s ancestor cards behind every group hanging on it (section 8.9), each as section 8.9 gives it. As in an ancestor card (step 2), a seam lies at the start of the first stretch, s1 × OL wide with the OL of the first anchor’s frame, and one at the end of the last stretch, sN × OR wide with the OR of the last anchor’s frame, N the last anchor and sN its group’s scale; a position at either lies on its stretch’s side. A position t left of the first stretch in no gap lies at XM(t) + s1 × (DL − OL), DL the sum of the shrinks of those gaps that start at or after t, and one right of the last stretch at XN(t) − sN × (DR − OR), DR the sum of those that end at or before t, each sum taken from the left. Between the first stretch and the last the arrangement’s gaps leave out what they leave out whatever the mode, and stored card elisions lying there are kept and not drawn; the gaps of each anchor’s frame lie in its run, carried as section 8.9 carries them.

Drawing a once card. The once card draws the layout of step 1 with every ordinate y of it at (yR − aR) + s1 × y, and every abscissa by the position it belongs to: an element at a position t of R’s axis, or at an offset o from it (a wedge’s corners, a label centred on it, a mark at a line’s end), lies at X(t) + s1 × o, X the run the position lies on; a line or row crossing a gap is drawn in pieces (section 6.6, Elisions), and one crossing a seam whole across it (section 3.2); the names, the chevrons and everything else of the layout left of R’s start s0' keep their distance from it at X(start) + s1 × (x − s0'), so the card’s left edge lies at X(start) − s1 × s0'; and the card’s right edge lies at X(end) + s1 × ((Ri' + K) − xe'), xe' the x of R’s end in the layout of step 1. Where R starts where the first stretch starts, its start lies on the seam there, on the stretch’s side, while its names and everything else of the layout left of s0' keep their distance from X(start) − s1 × OL, outside the first anchor’s frame, as a card behind one frame keeps its names outside it (step 2), so that the card’s left edge lies at X(start) − s1 × (OL + s0'). Every length of it, a stroke width, a font size, a mark, is s1 times its length there. The rows are those of step 1, as gaps never move a row (section 3.2). The once card is the ancestor card of R behind G1; R’s ancestor cards behind the other groups hanging on it draw nothing and take no room. A once card that is not displayed draws nothing but keeps its place in 1 to 4, so that hiding it moves nothing.

8.9 Elisions#

Where. Elisions (section 2) are drawn where their timeline is drawn whole. A card elision, one on a timeline that lies in no group, is drawn in the cards that draw its root whole: the ungrouped root’s card and every ancestor card of that root (section 8.8), and for a virtual timeline, its own root, its placed card (section 8.10); never in a member’s card, whose members convert linearly between their shared start and end (README, “Groups and the graphical object”). A group elision, one on a timeline that lies in a group, is drawn across the frame of each group its timeline lies in, at one place in every member card of that frame, and carried into the ancestor cards behind that frame (below); an ancestor of a member lies in no group through it (section 2), so an elision on the root of a member, or on an ancestor between, is a card elision of that root. A root that is a member of one group and holds a member of another lies wholly in the first, so all its elisions are group elisions of that frame, and its ancestor cards behind other frames have no card elision. An elision is drawn where its gap is displayed (section 2.4); a group elision also only where its timeline’s line is drawn in the frame, as a groupstamp’s lines across a frame are (section 6.6).

Modes. Which elisions a card draws is the card’s mode, its elision-mode (section 2.4), cascaded as every property is; every card of a root R, its ungrouped card and its ancestor cards, carries the address {"id": R, "part": ["card"]}, so one rule addressed to it sets the mode of all of them, beside the display that hides them:

elision-mode

What the card draws

full

no card elision, and no derived one

proportional

the elisions the card derives (Derived elisions, below), and no stored card elision

custom

the stored card elisions lying on R or on a descendant of R, and no derived one

auto (initial)

as custom where at least one stored card elision lies on R or a descendant, displayed or not, and as proportional otherwise

Group elisions are never counted and never switched by a card’s mode: an ancestor card draws the gaps carried into it in every mode, so a group elision neither turns auto into custom nor disappears under full; a card that is not displayed draws nothing at all. An ungrouped root’s card has no protected span and derives nothing, so proportional draws no gap there. elision-proportion and elision-depths act under proportional alone, and elision-display is the display of every elision the card derives (none an empty one), the order and repetition of its words meaning nothing. A frame reads elision-mode for its group elisions as an ungrouped root’s card reads it for its own: full draws none, neither across the frame nor carried, auto and custom draw them, and proportional draws none; it reads none of the other three. Pieces. A card elision’s stretch on the root’s axis runs from its start to its end, each carried to the root as section 3.1 carries a coordinate, and no further than the root’s start and end (section 3.4). In an ancestor card aligned to a member M, the protected span, M’s span on the root’s axis from −ts to −ts + λ (section 8.8), is never elided by a card elision. The card elisions a card draws are taken in canonical order (section 2), and each draws as its pieces what of its stretch lies outside the protected span and outside every piece taken before it: none, one or several stretches, each with the elision’s address, name, width and display. So an elision overlapping the protected span is clipped to what lies outside it, drawn as up to two pieces; of two elisions that overlap, the earlier is drawn whole and the later clipped; a piece without length is not drawn; and nothing is refused. A stored elision the card does not draw, its gap not displayed or its timeline not drawn there (Where, above), has no pieces and takes no stretch from the elisions after it. Each piece of a stored elision is one gap of section 3.2 over the whole piece, its elided coordinates the piece’s ends on the elision’s timeline’s own axis.

Widths. A gap of a stored elision over [a, b] has the natural width n of section 3.2 and the drawn width set by its elision’s width (section 2): when it is absent, the standard gap 32; for {"ratio": q}, q × n; for {"span": v}, L × uv, uv = v / λR a proportion formed as section 3.2 forms one and λR the root’s length, or its reach when its length is null; for {"canvas": c}, c; each in binary64, and n instead when it is larger than n. L is the card’s graphical length, LA in an ancestor card (section 8.8), and a width is in the card’s item coordinates, so its item’s scale scales it. Each piece of a clipped elision takes its width so, as if it were the whole elision.

Group elisions. Let M be the member of a group G that is the elision’s timeline T or has it as a descendant, and λ M’s length, or its reach when its length is null (section 3.4). The elision’s start and end, carried to M’s axis as section 3.1 carries a coordinate to a root, become the proportions p and q of λ (section 3.2), each clipped to [0, 1]: the elision’s stretch in the frame. The frame’s group elisions are taken in canonical order, and each draws as its pieces what of its stretch lies outside every piece taken before it, one the frame does not draw (Where, above) taking no stretch; each piece is one gap of the frame’s elided axis (section 3.2), with the natural width n = L × q − L × p and the drawn width set by its elision’s width as above, {"span": v} giving L × uv with uv = v / λ, and {"canvas": c} c in the frame’s item coordinates, so the frame’s scale scales it; n instead when it is larger. One stored width thus sets the gap of every member card at once, and a timeline lying in two groups has a gap in each frame, each by that frame’s L and scale. The frame is drawn shorter by DG, the sum of the shrinks of its gaps (section 8.2), its members’ drawn length L' = L − DG. A gap runs across the frame’s member cards, from the top of the topmost to the bottom of the bottommost (section 6.6); every row of every member card that crosses it stops and resumes there, and the gap draws one ellipsis, centred on the member cards together, with its label stack once, below that ellipsis in its drawn tier (section 6.6), its room taken in the markers band of the elision’s own timeline T in its card in that frame (section 6.4, Markers) and nowhere else. Its labels show the piece’s ends on T’s own axis: the elision’s start and end where the piece is its whole stretch, neither clipped to [0, 1] nor cut by an earlier piece; on any other piece both ends are read on T at the piece’s proportions, p × λ and q × λ on M’s axis (where λ is exact, the piece’s exact proportions, defined under Carried gaps below, and the products exact; where λ is a float, the doubles p and q and the products in binary64), less T’s start on M’s axis, combined as section 3.1 combines values, and written as section 5.2 writes a coordinate. The gap’s elided coordinates on a member, or on a timeline its card draws, are the positions at the proportions p and q of that member’s drawn length, each the exact product of the piece’s exact proportion (Carried gaps, below) and that length, a float length taken at its exact binary value, read on the timeline and written in its number type as a groupstamp’s coordinates are (section 2). They have no value where a groupstamp’s coordinate would have none: on a member of unknown length, and on every member when M’s length is unknown; where a member has none, no timeline of its card has one. They are listed by an application and never drawn: card by card in the display order of the frame’s members, in each card its member, then the other timelines of the member’s tree the card draws, in traversal order (section 6.1), then the ancestors whose lines the card draws, from the member’s parent up to its root. A timeline other than the member is listed only where its span, its ends included, holds both positions, as a row crossing the gap does (section 6.6), an ancestor of unknown length having no end. The gap is a place of the drawing, not a coordinate, so nothing of it is faded. A position in a frame’s gap has no x (section 3.2): a groupstamp’s lines and wedges at such a position are not drawn, and at a gap’s edge they are.

Carried gaps. The ancestor card of R behind G, aligned to M (section 8.8), keeps M’s span on R over M’s line, so it draws the frame’s gaps inside its protected span: each gap of the frame over [p, q] becomes a gap of the card over R’s stretch from −ts + p × λ to −ts + q × λ, with the frame’s natural width n and drawn width g, taken and never recomputed, so that the card’s gaps lie over the frame’s, up to the rounding of binary64. Here p and q are the piece’s exact proportions: the exact quotients of the elision’s start and end, carried to M’s axis, and λ, each clipped to [0, 1] (a float taken at its exact binary value), not the doubles that place the frame’s gap; where an earlier piece cut the elision’s stretch, the cut end is the earlier piece’s exact end there, so that the carried gaps meet exactly where the frame’s gaps meet. On an exact axis of R the stretch is exact, −ts, p × λ and their sum computed exactly, and on a float axis it is combined as section 3.1 combines values, each exact operand converted to the nearest double; only its x is rounded (section 3.2). A carried gap is drawn where the frame draws its gap, by the frame’s mode and display and not by the card’s cascade, in every mode of R; it is a further drawing of the frame’s gap, carries its elision’s address and takes the values cascaded for the frame’s gap, ellipsis included, and it draws one ellipsis, centred on the ancestor card, and no label. Card elisions and derived ones are clipped around the protected span, so they never meet a carried gap, and everything right of M’s span lies OR further right and DG further left with the frame’s shared end (section 8.8, step 2). A gap of theirs whose edge falls on a seam at the protected span’s start or end ends or starts there on its own side, at the frame’s edge, and every row running on from it crosses the seam whole (section 3.2). Rows are untouched: the card’s ordinates are those of section 8.8.

Derived elisions. An ancestor card of a root R aligned to a member M derives elisions where its mode is proportional and its proportion r (below) is less than 1. Let A1 = R, …, Ak be M’s ancestors, Ak its parent (section 8.3). Depth j holds Aj and, for j ≥ 2, every other child of A(j − 1) the card draws (a displayed child, section 2.4; a segment without a line of its own counts when it is displayed); depth 1 holds R alone. The chosen depths are those of elision-depths: all of 1 … k for auto, otherwise the depths it lists that are at most k. From the deepest chosen depth up, and within a depth in canonical order (section 6.1), each stretch of a timeline’s span (from its start to its end, sections 3.1 and 3.4) that lies outside the protected span, outside the spans of the timelines of the deeper chosen depths and outside every piece found before becomes one piece lying on that timeline, its ends on that timeline’s own axis. A stretch no chosen depth covers stays at full scale. So with auto M’s parent and its siblings take what they cover of R, what they leave uncovered falls to the next depth up and R takes the rest, and with "1" alone R is elided once on each side of the protected span, its pieces taken from R’s own span and their gaps cutting every row of the card that crosses them, its children’s rows included (section 6.6).

Each piece is drawn as its middle left out: a piece over [a, b] of its timeline’s own axis, [aR, bR] on the root’s axis, with the natural width n (section 3.2, with LA), is drawn W = r × n wide as a visible start, a gap and a visible end.   1. its elided coordinates are start, the larger of a and ⌊a + h / k⌋, and end, the smaller of b and ⌈b − h / k⌉, each sum combined as section 3.1 combines values and rounded outwards to a whole unit of the timeline, an integer, which every number type holds exactly: int as the integer, float as the double equal to it and fraction as [n, 1];

  2. the visible ends keep their natural widths, e1 from aR to start and e2 from end to bR, each on the root’s axis (section 3.2), both 0 where start = a and end = b;

  3. the gap over [start, end] is drawn g = W − e1 − e2 wide, in binary64 left to right, so that the piece stays W wide up to the rounding of binary64.

8.10 Virtual timelines and switched cards#

A FlowMap (README, “FlowMaps and virtual timelines”) is drawn as the axis it plays, its virtual axis: by the card of a virtual timeline that applies it, a virtual card, and by the card of its own timeline where the card’s flow names it, a switched card. Both draw the chunks of the FlowMap alike. Nothing here reads flow control or computes a flow: every position comes from the FlowMap’s chunks by the arithmetic of section 3.1.

Resolution. A FlowMap F whose timeline names a root R is resolved into a list of pieces on R’s axis, taking the entries of its chunks in their order; the entry at index k, from 0, gives

  1. for a gap {gap: g}: one gap piece, of the length g;

  2. for the ID of a region X of a timeline T of R’s tree, R itself included: one stretch [s + X.start, s + X.end], s the start of T on R’s axis (section 3.1);

  3. for the ID of a child timeline C at any depth: one stretch [s, s + C.length], s the start of C on R’s axis;

  4. for the ID of a virtual timeline W: the pieces W resolves to, below, in their order.

Every piece belongs to the entry that gave it, so the pieces of a virtual timeline named as a chunk are one entry. F is unresolved where its timeline names no root, where an entry names a child of unknown length or a virtual timeline that does not resolve, and where it is reached again while it is resolved, through the virtual timelines its entries name. A virtual timeline V resolves where its map is a FlowMap that resolves, its timeline is that FlowMap’s timeline and it states no through, to that FlowMap’s pieces; every other virtual timeline, one applying a conversion map, one stating through, one applied to another timeline, is unresolved.

The pieces lie end to end on the virtual axis from 0: the first starts at P0 = 0, and each further one where the one before it ends, P(i + 1) = Pi + ℓi, ℓi the length of piece i, b − a for a stretch [a, b] and g for a gap, each sum combined as section 3.1 combines values; the played length Λ of F is the end of the last piece, 0 where there is none. An entry’s span runs from the start of its first piece to the end of its last.

Runs. A run is a longest sequence of n ≥ 2 consecutive entries naming one ID; gaps form none. In a card that is an item of the block, never in a frame, a run is drawn once: its first entry is drawn, and the rest of its span, from the end of its first entry a to the end of its last b, is a run gap, a gap of section 3.2 over [a, b] whose drawn width is 0, so that its whole natural width is its shrink: the run takes the drawn width of one entry and every position after it lies that shrink further left, as after an elision. The shrink is not subtracted, though: a position t at or after the end b of a run gap, compared exactly, and before the start of the next one has the x

x(t) = x(a) + (L × u − L × ub − D)

x(a) the x of the gap’s start, by this same rule where an earlier run gap lies before it, ub the proportion of b, the two products formed first, and D the sum of the shrinks of the gaps of elisions lying on the card’s virtual timeline (Elisions of a virtual timeline, below) from b up to t, those with b ≤ a' and b' ≤ t for a gap over [a', b'], compared exactly and summed from the left as section 3.2 sums them, 0 where there is none; the end of the line, at u = 1, so lies at x(a) + (L − L × ub − D) for the last run gap, D the shrinks of every gap of an elision after it. So x(b) = x(a) exactly, and the entry after a run starts where the run’s drawn entry ends, which L × u − D would miss by the rounding of D. A run gap is no elision: it has no address, draws nothing, neither rectangle, ellipsis, continuation nor label, and cuts no row, a row that crosses it being drawn across it whole as across a seam (section 3.2); a position in it, a < t < b compared exactly, is not drawn and takes no room. A card with run gaps lays out every first fit (section 6.3) by the drawn x less x0, not by L × u, so that the rows follow the drawn widths. In a frame every entry is drawn: the members of a group share one linear axis (section 8.2), which a gap of one member’s runs would break.

The virtual card. A virtual timeline V is drawn as the root of its member drawing (section 8.1): in a card of its own where it is a placed virtual timeline, an item of the block at the place places states under its ID, and in the frame of each group that has it as a member. Removing the ID from places hides the card and keeps the virtual timeline. Its axis is its own, from 0 to its length, its λ of section 3.2 that length, and the positions of its named timestamps their coordinates. Where V resolves, the entries of its FlowMap lie on that axis at their spans, whatever V’s length states (a valid manifest states their sum where the axis is exact). Its card draws

  • its line, a timeline element with the address {"id": V} in the style of V’s unit, from x(0) to x(λ), with the start circle and, its end fixed by its map, the end circle of a locked root (section 6.6), never an arrowhead or chevrons; over the span of every gap entry the line is not drawn and a gap line stands in for it (section 6.6, Gap lines), the line written as its pieces from the left;

  • its chunks, in its regions band (section 6.4, Regions): each drawn entry k a brace over its drawn span, for a run drawn once over its first entry’s span, a region element with the address {"id": V, "facet": "regions", "part": [k]}, and a gap entry no brace; each entry its label, a label element {"id": V, "facet": "regions", "part": [k, "label"]} placed as a region’s label (section 6.6, Regions) with its leader {"id": V, "facet": "regions", "part": [k, "leader"]} where it moves to a further tier. The label is the name of the entry’s source: a region’s name, or its whole ID where the name is null, so that every chunk is labelled; a child timeline’s or a virtual timeline’s name (section 5.3); and for a gap the pretty text of its length on R’s axis (section 5.2: 2 ♩, 5.764″). The label of a run drawn once is its first entry’s followed by a space, × (U+00D7), a space and n in decimal digits, in the label’s own font size and place, so that a loop of four bars played eight times is labelled Loop × 8. A chunk’s brace matches {"element": "region"} and the rules selecting its address, never a class (it has no type statement), so the default hiding atomic sections hides no chunk whose source is one;

  • its boundaries: at the drawn start of every entry k ≥ 1 that is not the second or a later entry of a run drawn once, a boundary element {"id": V, "facet": "regions", "part": [k, "start"]} drawn as a segment’s (section 6.6, Boundaries), held by the line and hidden with it, not with the regions band, and reaching the boundary half-height 8 on both sides of the line (section 6.4, Level 0);

  • its named timestamps, in its markers band, with their cross-section lines, and in a frame its groupstamps across the frame, as every root’s;

  • its name (section 6.6, Names): the name of V (section 5.3) followed by · unfolded from and the name of its timeline where its map is a FlowMap, and by · converted from and that name where it is a conversion map, · being U+00B7: Allegro, default · unfolded from Allegro. A rule setting the name’s content replaces the whole text.

A virtual timeline has no children, conversion maps or flow control, so its children, conversion maps and flow control bands are never present; its band order is its entry of graphical.order, else the default of section 4. It lies in no tree: it has no ancestor line and no ancestor card. A new virtual timeline is so drawn stripped down: it lends nothing of the bands of the timeline it comes from, unlike a switched card (below). An unresolved virtual timeline draws its line whole, its named timestamps, its elisions and its name, and no chunk, boundary or gap line.

Elisions of a virtual timeline. An elision may lie on a virtual timeline V (section 2). Its start and end are positions on V’s own axis, the played axis from 0 to V’s length, never on the axis of the timeline V comes from: it leaves out a stretch of the playing, and nothing of the written timeline, so a stretch played twice is left out only where the elision lies, and no other card of that timeline draws it. V is the root of its member drawing and lies in no tree, so an elision on it is a group elision of each group V is a member of, drawn across that group’s frame (section 8.9, Group elisions), and otherwise a card elision of V’s placed card, drawn as that card’s elision-mode selects (section 8.9, Modes): a placed virtual card, like an ungrouped root’s card, has no protected span and derives nothing, so proportional draws no gap there, and auto and custom draw the stored ones. A card elision of V is taken as section 8.9 takes one (Pieces, Widths), on V’s axis with λ V’s length, except that its pieces are what of its stretch lies outside every run gap of the card as well: a run gap is drawn 0 wide already, and an elision leaves nothing out there. Each piece is a gap of section 3.2 and cuts what crosses it as every gap does (section 6.6, Elisions): the line and its gap lines are drawn in pieces, a chunk’s brace is cut as a region’s is, and is not drawn, with its label, where its start, end or midpoint lies in the gap, and a boundary or a named timestamp marking a position in the gap is not drawn. A card switched to V (below) draws none of V’s elisions: it draws its own timeline, and no elision at all.

So, in the worked example below, an elision of virtual_1 from [10, 1] to [14, 1] on its placed card (L = 400) lies wholly in the run gap [8, 16] of entries 0 and 1: it has no piece and draws no gap. One from [17, 1] to [19, 1] with the width {"canvas": 8} lies in entry 2: its one piece has the natural width 400 × u(19) − 400 × u(17) = 316.66666666666663 − 283.33333333333337 = 33.33333333333326, the drawn width 8 and the shrink 25.333333333333258; it lies after the run gap, so x(17) = x0 + (133.33333333333331 + (283.33333333333337 − 266.66666666666663 − 0)) = x0 + 150.00000000000006 and x(19) = x0 + (133.33333333333331 + (316.66666666666663 − 266.66666666666663 − 25.333333333333258)) = x0 + 158.00000000000006; the brace of B, whose midpoint 20 lies in no gap, is cut there, the gap draws no ellipsis (it is narrower than 20), and the line ends at `x(24) = x0 + (133.33333333333331

  • (400 − 266.66666666666663 − 25.333333333333258)) = x0 + 241.33333333333343` in its end circle.

Switched cards. A card of a timeline T that reads flow (section 2.4), a member’s card or the card of an ungrouped root, is switched where its cascaded flow names a FlowMap F of T that resolves, or a virtual timeline that resolves to such a FlowMap; it is drawn as written otherwise. A switched card is laid out by section 6 on F’s virtual axis, λ being Λ:

  • its root line is drawn as a virtual card’s line, from 0 to Λ, with its gap lines, its chunks and their labels in its regions band before T’s regions, and their boundaries, each carrying T’s ID in place of V’s ({"id": T, "facet": "regions", "part": [k]});

  • it keeps the band configuration of the card drawn as written: every band of T and of every timeline of its tree, the children, markers, regions, conversion maps and flow control bands alike, is laid out by section 6.4, displayed or hidden, in its band order and in its cascaded style as it is there, so that a band hidden on the card drawn as written is hidden on the switched card, and hides what it holds (section 2.4);

  • every element those bands hold, and every segment’s boundary and label on a line of T’s tree, is unfolded: drawn in every stretch piece whose written stretch holds it, at its played positions. A position t on T’s axis (section 3.1) lies in a stretch piece [a, b] starting at P where a ≤ t < b, or where t = b and that piece is the last or is followed by a gap or by a stretch that does not start at b, and is played there at P + (t − a), combined as section 3.1 combines values; a position in no stretch is not drawn. An element at a position, a flow-control element with its flow line, symbol, fork and stack, a named timestamp’s wedge with its labels and cross-section line, a segment’s boundary, is drawn at every played position of its position, each drawing with its labels. An element over [s, e], a child’s line, a region, a map over the extent of its timeline (sections 3.1 and 3.4), is drawn once in every stretch piece where max(s, a) < min(e, b), over the played positions of those two ends, so clipped to the piece, keeping its start mark only where max(s, a) = s and its end mark only where min(e, b) = e, as a row cut at an elision (section 6.6, Elisions); an element over a stretch that no piece reaches into is not drawn. A region so clipped is drawn as a whole brace over its played stretch, with its whole label centred on that brace’s played midpoint in every drawing (section 6.6, Regions), never a part of its name; a child’s line so clipped keeps its one name in the name column (below); a map so clipped is a closed double line over its played stretch; a segment’s label is placed over each drawing of its segment as a clipped region’s label is over its brace. An arc of a jump is drawn in a piece only where its from and its to both lie in that piece, from the played x of the one to that of the other, with its label, as the flow control band of a member that is a child timeline draws no arc whose to lies outside its stretch (section 6.4, Gathering): a jump played in a piece that does not hold its to draws its symbol, its fork and its stack there and no arc. Within one piece the drawings keep the tree of section 2.1, a child’s drawing holding the drawings in that piece of what the child holds, and section 6 lays each drawing out as one item, the conversion maps’ slots and the flow rows included, the drawings taken piece by piece, in the order of the pieces, and within a piece as section 6 takes the elements. Every drawing keeps its element’s address (section 2.2), and a label of a coordinate shows the written coordinate, never the played one. A row of the name column holding several drawings of one timeline names it once (section 6.6, Names). An alignment anchor joins the nearest pair of its timestamps’ cross-section segments (section 8.7);

  • no gap of a stored, derived or group elision (section 8.9), and none of an elision lying on the virtual timeline flow may name;

  • its name is the row name of T followed by · and the name of the FlowMap or virtual timeline flow names, its name or, where that is null, its whole ID.

Runs are drawn once on the switched card of an ungrouped root and on no card in a frame (Runs, above).

So switching keeps what the card shows, where a new virtual timeline’s card starts stripped down (The virtual card, above). A child of allegro over [450, 520], were one added, crosses the ends 953/2 and 500 of the stretches: it is drawn over [450, 953/2] in piece 0 with its start circle, over [953/2, 500] in piece 1 with no mark, over [950, 1953/2] in piece 2 with its start circle and over [1953/2, 1993/2] in piece 3 with its end mark, and named once in its row.

Switched frames. A frame is switched where exactly one of its members T has a switched card in it: every member card of the frame is drawn on T’s FlowMap F, every member carried through the group. A position m on the own axis of a member M goes to T’s axis at t = (m × λT) / λM, λM and λT the lengths of M and T or their reaches where unknown (section 3.4): exact where all three are exact, and otherwise in binary64, each exact operand taken as the nearest double and the product formed first. It is the position a groupstamp’s line marks on T (section 6.6, Cross-section lines), and unfolds there as above; an extent of M goes so at both ends, and M’s own line, from 0 to its end, is drawn as a child’s line is, once in every stretch piece it reaches into, its start circle and its end mark kept only where they lie in that piece. Every member drawing of the frame then lies on F’s virtual axis from x0 to x0 + L (section 8.2) and draws its own tree unfolded, its ancestor lines included, keeping the band configuration of its card drawn as written as a switched card does (above): every band of its tree as written, flow control and conversion maps included, every element they hold carried to T’s axis and then unfolded, an arc drawn in a piece only where its from and its to, both carried, lie in that piece. In groups/switched_frame the card of allegro switches the frame of performance to default: allegro’s card in the frame draws its flow control, and its atomic sections where a rule shows them, at the played positions of the worked case above, and recording (702.5 seconds) draws its line once in each of the four pieces, a position m of it carried to t = (m × 1644) / 702.5 in binary64, so that a timestamp at 351.25 would lie at 822.0 and be played in piece 3 at 976.5 + (822.0 − 500) = 1298.5. Only T’s card draws the chunks of F, their boundaries and its gap lines, a virtual member’s own chunks unfolding as every other element of its drawing, and over a gap of F every other member draws nothing. An ancestor line of a member M that is a child timeline (section 8.3) is unfolded as M’s own line is: the stretch of M’s axis it is drawn over, from 0 to te where te < λ and to λ otherwise, goes to T’s axis at both ends and is drawn once in every stretch piece it reaches into, from the played x of its start there to that of its end, keeping its start only where max(s, a) = s and its end only where min(e, b) = e; a start or end so kept is drawn as section 8.3 draws it, with the played x of 0 in place of x0 (a line running on to the left starts 4w before it, in chevrons) and the played x of its end in place of x0 + L' and of x(te) (a line running on to the right ends 4w after it, in chevrons), and an end not kept draws no mark. The named timestamps on it and the secondary coordinates on it are drawn at every played position of their positions on M’s axis, labelled with the written coordinates, their stacks placed by its one first fit piece by piece, in the order of the pieces, and within a piece in the order of section 8.3. The start s0 of section 8.2 still counts every ancestor line that runs on to the left. A groupstamp’s lines cross the frame at every played x of its position. A switched frame draws no group elision and carries none into the ancestor cards behind it, which are drawn as written. Where two or more members of a group have switched cards, which both validators warn of (flow_conflict), its frame and all its cards are drawn as written: a frame has one axis, and no member’s flow outranks another’s.

Overlays. The flow subeditor’s pane, which an editor folds out under the card it edits, is an overlay of the editor and no element of a drawing; SVG and TikZ never write it. The translucent twin a drag lifts out of that pane is such an overlay too. The ribbons that join a chunk to its stretch on its source’s card are such overlays as well, like the coordinate flag.

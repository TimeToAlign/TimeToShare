Describing what a manifest is about#

This specification states how a TimeToShare manifest says what its elements stand for and how those things relate to others: by property–value statements in published vocabularies, optionally annotated with their provenance, and read as linked data through the manifest’s inline JSON-LD context. It covers the two planes of a manifest (section 1), the subject of a statement (section 2), the bibliographic classes of the things a manifest holds (section 3), pairs and their values (section 4), “is a” (section 5), vocabularies and patterns (section 6), values that name elements (section 7), YAML-LD and the inline context (section 8), provenance and beliefs (section 9), the catalogue of identifier systems, verbs and types (section 10), the package functions that edit statements (section 11) and validation (section 12). Section 13 lists the statement fixtures.

The definitions Context, Statements and StatementValue of the manifest’s schema document and the provenance entry of its provenance document, provenance.schema.json, with the definitions they use (PrefixName, PrefixIri, Iri, IriReference, LanguageTag, Types, Values, SingleValue, OrderedValues, ValueObject, ValueNode, Provenance, ProvenanceEntry, EntryValue and the belief), state the format normatively; Context, Statements, StatementValue and ProvenanceEntry are public, and so is the provenance document as a whole (README, “The schema document”). The same provenance entry annotates the rows of the MatchClaim table (README, “Provenance and beliefs”); this specification states it for statements. Terms in code are JSON members. Statements hold no events, and neither the drawing (docs/drawing.md) nor any rule of the temporal structure reads them.

The examples are excerpts of the statement fixtures under fixtures/0.2.0/statements/, written in canonical YAML (README, “Serializations”), with the format’s context left out. Their Turtle readings take the manifest to lie at https://example.org/manifests/<name>.yaml and write the empty prefix : for that address followed by #; the other prefixes are those of the manifest’s context.

1. Two planes#

A manifest holds two kinds of information. Its temporal structure (timelines and their nesting, groups, conversion maps, MeasureMaps, regions and flow control) states how coordinates relate: which axis a position lies on, where a child lies on its parent, how one unit converts into another, and in which order passages are played. Its statements state what a thing is and how it relates to other things: that a timeline is the axis of a video, who performs in it, which work it records, and where a statement came from. Each plane does its own work. A statement never nests, orders or aligns timelines, and the temporal structure never says what an element is: a child timeline is a stretch of its parent’s axis and implies no parthood, and a statement that one thing is part of another implies nothing about the timelines.

2. The subject#

Three kinds of element may carry statements, each in an object statements, with their annotations in a list provenance (section 9): the manifest as a whole, every timeline at any depth (children and segments included) and every region. No other element carries statements: a group, a conversion map, a MeasureMap, a flow-control element, a FlowMap and a named timestamp have none, and the validators refuse the two keys on a group (section 12). A flow-control element and a FlowMap carry provenance alone, whose entries are about the element itself and name no pair (section 9); they are no subjects of statements.

As the subject of statements, each of these elements stands for a thing:

  • the bundle, the manifest itself, for the package it is, which names the work it is about, a sonata or a DJ set, by schema:about and is never that work;

  • a timeline for the manifestation it is the time axis of: this edition, this encoding, this scan, this recording file, this video;

  • a region for the stretch of that manifestation it marks, such as a movement of a recording or a track of a set.

The subject’s IRI is the manifest’s address with the element’s ID as its fragment, <manifest address>#<id> (README, “Identity”), and an element carrying statements must have an ID that is an IRI fragment (section 12). A manifest without id is a blank node as a subject, which no other document can name; the validators warn of it (bundle_blank).

3. Work, expression, manifestation, item#

The things a manifest holds are classified after the IFLA Library Reference Model and its object-oriented form LRMoo. A manifest bundles the manifestations of one or more expressions of one work, including the expressions that work’s expression aggregates or incorporates.

Class

The thing

In a manifest

work

a creation in the abstract, intangible and without fixed bounds

never packaged; a value node, named by the bundle’s schema:about and by a timeline’s “example of work”

expression

one realisation of a work: the notated text of a version, a recorded performance

a value node, named by a timeline’s “embodies”; a performance is an event, and its recording an expression of its own

manifestation

one embodiment of an expression: an edition, an encoding, a scan of a score; a release, an upload, an audio file, a video

the thing a timeline is the axis of, each timeline of exactly one

item

one exemplar of a manifestation: a copy in a library, a file at an address

a value node on its manifestation’s timeline, holding its location and, for a file, its digest

A work has no time axis, and a manifest never delineates one; an expression has none of its own either. Works and expressions therefore occur only as values. The bundle names its work by schema:about and is typed as nothing that is a work; a timeline names what it manifests, an expression by lrmoo:R4_embodies, a work by Wikidata’s wdt:P629 or schema.org’s schema:exampleOfWork. Such a value node states its own “is a” and its identity with an authority’s IRI by “same as”. The “is a” of a timeline or a region names a manifestation class or a kind of manifestation, never a class of work or expression (section 5). Whatever has no axis in the manifest stays a value node.

An edition, an encoding and a scan of the score are manifestations of the notated expression; a release, an upload or a file of a recording are manifestations of the recorded expression. A copy is an item, a value node on its manifestation’s timeline that holds its location and, for a file, its digest; for a digital carrier the location is the item statement. A compound is itself the one work. A disc is an aggregate whose release embodies the compilation’s expression and the pieces’ expressions with it, so the disc’s timeline and its tracks’ children are axes of that one manifestation. A DJ set is a work of its own whose recorded expression incorporates the tracks’ expressions in altered form. Its timeline is the axis of the set’s recording, which names the set by “recording of” (wdt:P2550); the tracks’ own timelines, where a manifest holds them, are manifestations of those expressions and relate to the set by alignment alone.

The four classes are types of published vocabularies, and the format has no key for them: a manifest states them where it wants them, as type: ["lrmoo:F3_Manifestation"] or type: ["melod:Manifestation"] (section 5). In nielsen_record, a catalogue record of Carl Nielsen’s song CNW 133, the bundle is about the catalogue’s work, which is realised in one expression. The print and the autograph are manifestations, each a timeline; the print embodies the expression, a value node. The one copy of the autograph, held by the Royal Danish Library under the shelfmark CNS 342e, is a candidate for its exemplar on the autograph’s timeline, the library’s siglum awaiting reconciliation (abridged):

4. Pairs#

A statement is a pair of a property and a value, both technical identifiers where they name things. The container statements holds an element’s pairs: each key is type (section 5) or a property IRI, written as a compact IRI prefix:suffix with a prefix of the manifest’s context (section 8) or as an absolute IRI; each value is the non-empty list of that property’s values, without repeats and in the order given. Writers keep that order, and canonical order (README, “Canonical order”) moves no value and no entry of provenance. A container is never empty.

A value is one of four kinds:

Value

Written

Read as

a literal

a string, an integer or a boolean: "op. 106", 1, true

a plain literal, an xsd:integer or an xsd:boolean

a value object

@value with @language or with @type: {"@value": "Hr. Olufs Sang", "@language": da}, {"@value": "2025-12-05", "@type": "xsd:date"}

a literal with a language or a datatype

a node

an object with id, type, label, its own pairs and its own provenance, each optional, at least one present

a thing: the IRI of its id, or a blank node without one

an ordered list

{list: [...]} of single values

an RDF list

A string is never read as an IRI: a thing is always a node, {id: …}. A statement value is never a float and never null; a number with a fractional part is a value object with its datatype, as {"@value": "33.35", "@type": "xsd:decimal"}. A node’s label is a cache of the thing’s name, kept for display; the thing’s own source remains authoritative for it. A node’s own pairs are written directly as its keys, so a node is described as an element is. A node whose id names an element of the manifest holds that id alone (section 7).

The literals of op106_work are pairs of the work the bundle is about, a value node: a catalogue number in GND’s ontology and the work’s IMSLP page name, Wikidata’s external-identifier property holding a string (abridged):

statements:
  "schema:about":
    -
      "gndo:opusNumericDesignationOfMusicalWork": ["op. 106"]
      id: "mbw:4a622b0d-b0c0-405e-b49e-05b70c108284"
      label: "Piano Sonata No. 29, Op. 106"
      "wdt:P839": ["Piano_Sonata_No.29,_Op.106_(Beethoven,_Ludwig_van)"]

and the encoding of layerit_recording states its encoding date as a typed literal, "dcterms:created": [{"@type": "xsd:date", "@value": "2025-12-05"}].

5. “Is a”#

The first statement about a thing says what kind of thing it is, in as many vocabularies as wanted. A vocabulary of classes is stated with type, JSON-LD’s @type, whose values are class IRIs; a system that types by property is stated with its property, as Wikidata’s “instance of” wdt:P31. The video of layerit_recording is a video in schema.org’s terms, a manifestation in MeLODy’s and of the class “video” (wd:Q98069877) in Wikidata’s:

statements:
  type: ["schema:VideoObject", "melod:Manifestation"]
  "wdt:P31":
    -
      id: "wd:Q98069877"

On a timeline or a region every “is a” names a manifestation class or a kind of manifestation, as each of these three does; a class of work or expression is stated on the value node that stands for the work or the expression (section 3).

type holds one or more distinct IRIs and never a literal. In provenance, the pair type X is written with the property rdf:type and the value {id: X}; an entry giving the class as a literal string names no pair (section 9).

6. Vocabularies and patterns#

Each identifier system is used with the properties its own knowledge graph publishes: a Wikidata item with Wikidata’s properties (wdt:), a MusicBrainz ID with schema.org as MusicBrainz’s own JSON-LD writes it, a GND ID with the GND ontology (gndo:), a MeLODy entity with LRMoo and MeLODy’s terms, a library authority with the MARC relators (rel:). The same relation may therefore be stated in several vocabularies side by side, each with its own value. The video of layerit_recording names its performer twice, as Wikidata states performers and as libraries do, and ties the two values together by schema:sameAs inside the Wikidata node; the pairs are those of the fixture’s #cpt_1, the video of unknown length:

id: "#cpt_1"
length: null
statements:
  "rel:prf":
    -
      id: "viaf:113931950"
  "schema:sameAs":
    -
      id: "https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ch2mrPm1JnM"
  type: ["schema:VideoObject", "melod:Manifestation"]
  "wdt:P175":
    -
      id: "wd:Q235650"
      label: "Maria João Pires"
      "schema:sameAs":
        -
          id: "viaf:113931950"
        -
          id: "gnd:124927521"
  "wdt:P31":
    -
      id: "wd:Q98069877"
unit: seconds

A JSON-LD processor reads it as these triples, the timeline’s unit and length yielding none:

:cpt_1 a schema:VideoObject, melod:Manifestation ;
    rel:prf viaf:113931950 ;
    schema:sameAs <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ch2mrPm1JnM> ;
    wdt:P175 wd:Q235650 ;
    wdt:P31 wd:Q98069877 .

wd:Q235650 rdfs:label "Maria João Pires" ;
    schema:sameAs viaf:113931950, gnd:124927521 .

The file holds properties only. A relation in words, “performed by” or “is a recording of”, is a verb of the catalogue (section 10), which maps each verb and identifier system to a pattern, the properties that express the verb in that system’s vocabulary with a slot for the value. A plain pair is the simplest pattern (wdt:P175 with a thing); some vocabularies need a small node, as MusicBrainz states a performer by a schema:contributor node holding the agent and the role, and MeLODy by a melod:Contribution. Tools use the catalogue to offer the properties of a verb and to print a pair in words; a property the catalogue does not know is as valid as any other.

7. Values that name elements#

A node whose id begins with # names an element of the same manifest, {id: "#cpt_1"}, which reads as <manifest address>#cpt_1, the IRI of that element as a subject. An element of another manifest is named by that manifest’s address with the ID as fragment, relative (other.yaml#clt_1) or absolute. Any property may take such a value; “derived from”, relating one manifestation to another, is the usual case. A work or an expression is never such a value, since no element stands for one (section 3). A node naming an element of the manifest holds its id alone: the element describes itself through its own statements, so a label or pair beside the id would describe it a second time.

These values are IRI references, written and held alike. A reader that strips the # from IDs and references of the temporal structure (README, “Written in a file”) leaves every IRI inside statements and provenance as written, and the validators resolve each # reference there against every ID of the manifest, whatever its kind (section 12).

8. YAML-LD and the inline context#

A manifest is a YAML-LD document (W3C YAML-LD 1.0, media type application/ld+yaml): every manifest maps to JSON-LD 1.1, and canonical YAML is a subset of the YAML it reads. A manifest carrying statements or provenance holds the key @context at its top level and nowhere else: one object, the format’s terms each exactly as the schema fixes them and the prefixes its statements and provenance use. The context is inline, so the manifest is self-contained and a processor reads it without fetching anything. Writers write the prefixes used and no others, taking their namespaces from the manifest’s own context first and from the catalogue otherwise (Python’s with_context, npm’s withContext, section 11); every generated fixture of 0.2.0 holds a context, the 35 terms alone where it states nothing.

A prefix is a name beginning with a letter, followed by letters, digits, _ or -, which is no key of the manifest’s elements and no URI scheme the format reads as such (http, https, info, mailto, tag, urn); it stands for a namespace, an absolute IRI ending in / or #. A manifest may declare a prefix of its own beside those of the catalogue, as op106_work declares mbw for MusicBrainz works. A compact IRI whose prefix the context does not declare is refused (section 12); an absolute IRI is always valid, and an IRI of the schemes info, mailto, tag and urn is read as written.

The context writes each term with its full IRI, so the format itself needs no prefix. A key the context does not define, such as unit, length, name or graphical, yields nothing: a manifest without statements and provenance reads as the empty graph, and a manifest with them as their triples alone. Every term of a JSON-LD context is read wherever it stands, so an element key JSON-LD would read is refused there (reserved_key): a key beginning with @, @context on the manifest excepted; a key holding a colon; a term the element does not use, such as label on a timeline or statements on a group; and a prefix of the context. A timeline’s displayed name is therefore no rdfs:label of its subject, and a statement rdfs:label states one where wanted.

9. Provenance and beliefs#

A pair may be annotated with how it was made, its paradata (who stated it, when, how and on what basis), and with who believes it. The annotations of an element’s pairs are the entries of its provenance, a non-empty list beside its statements; the pairs of a value node are annotated in that node’s own provenance. A pair without an entry is stated by the manifest’s publisher. Each entry names its pair again, by its property and its value:

Key

Required

Holds

property

yes

the pair’s property, a compact or absolute IRI; the pair type X is written rdf:type

value

yes

the pair’s value: a literal, a value object, a thing by its id alone, or a node without id, compared as a whole

stated_by

no

the agents who stated it, IRIs or references

date

no

the day it was stated, YYYY-MM-DD

method

no

manual, reconciled (looked up in a service and matched), imported (taken over from a source document) or inferred (derived by a tool), or the IRI of another method

service

no

the lookup service, an IRI

basis

no

the sources it rests on: IRIs, or references resolved against the manifest’s address, such as #clt_6 or a file beside the manifest

match

no

how closely the value matches what was looked up: exact, close, broad, narrow or related

note

no

a note in words

beliefs

no

who believes the pair, a non-empty list of beliefs (below)

The same entry, without property and value, annotates a MatchClaim in the provenance column of the MatchClaim table (README, “Provenance and beliefs”), and a flow-control element or a FlowMap in its provenance (README, “Flow control”); provenance.schema.json defines it once for all of them. Such an entry is about the element itself: how it was made (imported by a loader, manual where a person added it, inferred) and who believes it. JSON-LD reads none of it, since flow_control and flow_maps are no terms of the context, so it needs the @context only for the prefixes it uses.

Stated pairs and candidates. A pair listed in statements is stated by the manifest, and its entry, where it has one, annotates it. An entry whose pair is not listed holds a candidate: a name imported without an identifier, a value a lookup suggested. A candidate is never stated: it exists only as its entry, and no processor reading the manifest asserts it. Stating a candidate lists its pair, and its entry then annotates the stated pair. A subject has at most one entry per pair.

Beliefs. A belief is {actor, belief, date, note?}: actor the person or organisation who holds it, an IRI, written absolute or as a reference resolved against the manifest’s address as in stated_by; belief one of the three strings "true", "false" and "unlikely", words like those of method and never JSON booleans; date the day it was stated, YYYY-MM-DD; and note a note in words. An actor holds at most one belief per entry: a later belief of the same actor replaces the earlier one, and one actor’s belief leaves every other actor’s standing. A belief is its actor’s alone. Nothing in the format derives a truth value from the beliefs, and nobody imposes one: a belief changes neither whether a pair is stated nor any other actor’s belief, so believing a stated pair false records the belief and leaves the pair stated; removing it is an edit of its own. One reading alone is fixed, outside the statements: a flow-control element that some actor believes false is rejected, and a reader computing a flow leaves it out (README, “Flow control”). A person who states a pair through an editor or the functions of section 11 is recorded with the belief true; a loader or a service records how the pair was made and holds no belief. A candidate an actor believes false is not offered to that actor again.

The belief filter selects, for one actor, the items whose state for that actor is one of the states it is given. An item has one of four states for an actor: the actor’s belief, true, unlikely or false, or undecided where the actor holds no belief on it. The filter keeps any set of these four states, by default every state but false; without an actor, nothing is filtered. Actors compare as the IRIs they stand for. Where an item holds beliefs of the actor under two spellings of one IRI, which a valid manifest never does and a MatchClaim table checked without its manifest may, its state is the most critical of them: false, then unlikely, then true. The filter selects the candidates an editor offers and the MatchClaims that give anchors (README, “From MatchClaims to anchors”) alike.

Which pair an entry names. An entry and a pair are the same pair when their properties are equal after expansion (type equals rdf:type) and their values are: literals equal with the same JSON type (true is not 1); value objects equal after their @type is expanded; a node with an id equal to any node with the same expanded id, its other keys ignored, a type item X counting as {id: X}; nodes without id equal as JSON once their own provenance is dropped; lists equal item by item. Relative references compare as written. Python’s same_pair and npm’s samePair decide it.

Reading. Each entry reads as RDF’s reification of its pair, a node with rdf:subject, rdf:predicate and rdf:object, the form RDF 1.2 turns into an annotation of a triple; JSON-LD 1.1 cannot produce a triple and its annotation from one object. Each belief reads as a node of its own under tts:beliefs. In layerit_recording the performer of the video was reconciled in Wikidata by an editor, who believes it, and the audio file is a candidate for being derived from the video, the value an element:

          id: "wd:Q235650"

      "wdt:P175":
        -
          id: "wd:Q235650"

    id: "#dpt_1"

      -

with tts: standing for the format’s namespace. The triple :dpt_1 dcterms:source :cpt_1 is absent: the candidate is reified and never asserted. In djset_segment, the segment’s sameAs to the original MusicBrainz recording is likewise a candidate, which an actor believes false, and nowhere asserted, and the ISRC of the recording the segment is based on is annotated on the node that states it, its reified statement having that thing, wd:Q65658996, as its subject. A node value without id in an entry, such as a name not yet reconciled, reads as a blank node of its own: the format identifies it with the pair by the comparison above, while RDF holds two distinct blank nodes.

10. The catalogue#

The catalogue is data shipped with both packages, catalogue/catalogue.yaml, written in canonical YAML with dumps, and described by its JSON Schema catalogue/catalogue.schema.json. Tools read it to offer properties and values and to print pairs in words. The validators never consult it: it guides a writer, and whether a property suits a subject is never refused. It holds five members:

  • prefixes: the namespaces of the vocabularies its patterns use, 21 in all (bf, cidoc, dcterms, gnd, gndo, lrmoo, melod, melodTerms, mus, owl, prov, rdf, rdfs, rel, schema, skos, viaf, wd, wdt, wdtn, xsd), from which writers take the prefixes a manifest’s context declares.

  • aliases: namespaces read as equal. https://schema.org/ is read as http://schema.org/, the form Wikidata, the GND, VIAF and MusicBrainz write.

  • systems: the identifier systems, each with its name, the prefixes of the vocabulary its linked data uses, how to look its things up (lookup, whose route is browser, server, cross_reference, database or none) and its things, the kinds of identifier it mints. A thing has an IRI template (http://www.wikidata.org/entity/{value}) or none, the URL patterns that recognise an address copied from the system’s site, each with one capturing group, whether its values are written in lower case, the URI of its identifier scheme and the LRMoo class of what it identifies, where one applies. The systems are Wikidata, MusicBrainz, the GND, VIAF, MeLODy with LRMoo, IMSLP, YouTube, ISRC, DOI and the fallback vocabularies (Dublin Core, schema.org, the MARC relators, BIBFRAME and others) that state a relation where a system has no property of its own.

  • verbs: the relations in words, is_a first, then same_as, recording_of, performed_by, conducted_by, composed_by, version_or_edition_of, exemplar_of, part_of, derived_from, released_on and recorded_at_on. A verb has a label, an inverse_label where it is read in both directions (“is part of”, “has part”), and per system a list of alternative patterns {pattern, when?, inverse?, note?}. A pattern is written as a statements container, every value a one-item list: the slot {id: "?"} takes a thing, "?" a literal or class IRI, and "?role", "?date", "?value" and "?position" named literals; inverse: true marks the inverse reading, and when limits an alternative to subjects of some types or values of some things.

  • types: the kinds of thing a subject or a value node is, each with a label, its “is a” patterns per system, the verbs usually stated of it in order and the systems to look its values up in: musical work, recorded performance, release, video, printed or scanned edition, score encoding and DJ set.

The verb “performed by” reads, in part:

id: performed_by
label: "performed by"
patterns:
  fallback:
    -
      pattern:
        "rel:prf":
          -
            id: "?"
  musicbrainz:
    -
      pattern:
        "schema:contributor":
          -
            "schema:contributor":
              -
                id: "?"
            "schema:roleName": ["?role"]
            type: ["schema:OrganizationRole"]
  wikidata:
    -
      pattern:
        "wdt:P175":
          -
            id: "?"

Both packages read it alike. Python’s load_catalogue() returns the shipped catalogue as a Catalogue, and read_catalogue(document) reads another, checking that no system, thing, verb or type is held twice, that every prefix it uses is declared, that every system, thing, verb and type it names exists and that every URL pattern has one capturing group; npm’s defaultCatalogue() and readCatalogue(document) do the same. Over a catalogue:

  • recognize(catalogue, url) returns the system, thing, value and IRI of an address copied from a system’s site, or none: https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q235650 gives the Wikidata item Q235650 and its IRI http://www.wikidata.org/entity/Q235650;

  • instantiate(catalogue, verb, system, value, …) returns the pairs that state a verb in a system, its slots filled; performed_by in Wikidata with wd:Q235650 gives ("wdt:P175", {"id": "wd:Q235650"});

  • verb_of (npm verbOf) returns the verb a pair states, and whether it states it inversely: rel:prf with a VIAF ID gives performed_by, dcterms:hasPart gives part_of read inversely;

  • rectangles(catalogue, manifest, subject) groups a subject’s pairs by verb and thing, the unit an editor displays: pairs of one verb, read the same way, form one group, transitively, when their values are one thing, through their ids and the IDs under their schema:sameAs and owl:sameAs, or when their value nodes carry the same string label, compared as written; “is a” comes always first. The two performer pairs of the video in section 6 form one group, and so do schema:exampleOfWork and wdt:P629 naming one work in two systems under one label.

11. Editing statements#

Both packages edit statements with pure functions that return a changed copy of a manifest, Python’s in timetoshare.statements and npm’s under camel-case names, both exported from the package root. A subject is addressed by its path in the manifest: () the bundle, a path ending in timelines, children or regions and an index an element, and a path through a container or a node’s key a value node. subject_path(manifest, id) returns the path of the timeline or region of an ID as written in the manifest, #cpt_1 in the file form, and None names the bundle.

Python

With provenance (the keys of an entry but property, value and beliefs) they are set on the pair’s entry, added where there is none; with actor, the person stating the pair, the entry records that actor’s belief true on date, replacing the actor’s earlier belief

without_pair(manifest, subject, property, value)

removes a pair and its entry; an emptied list, container or provenance is removed

with_candidate(manifest, subject, property, value, *, stated_by, date, method, service=None, basis=(), match=None, note=None)

npm’s withPair, withCandidate and withBelief take the keyword arguments in an options object, withPair(manifest, subject, property, value, { provenance, actor, date }), and candidates(manifest, subject, actor?) the actor last. Actors compare as their IRIs. Believing a pair false never removes it: the belief is recorded and the statement stays; without_pair removes a pair.

A function refuses a pair already stated ('<property>' already has this value), the removal of a pair not stated ('<property>' does not have this value), a candidate of a stated pair ('<property>' already has this value; a stated pair is no candidate), an actor given without a date (a belief needs a date), a belief none of the three words, a property that is neither type nor an IRI ('<property>' is no property: write 'type', a compact IRI or an absolute IRI), a path that addresses nothing (<pointer> addresses no element and no value node) and a date the calendar lacks ('<date>' is no Gregorian calendar date written YYYY-MM-DD), raising ValueError in Python and Error in npm with the same message. FORMAT_CONTEXT holds the 35 terms, NAMESPACE the format’s namespace, and BELIEFS, METHODS and MATCHES the words of the three keys.

12. Validation#

The validators read the version marker, then finite numbers, then the schema, the representation of own-axis values and the identity rules (README, “Identity”), and last the statement rules. The schema refuses, with the code schema:

  • a context that lacks one of the 35 terms or alters one, a prefix whose name is an element key or a URI scheme the format reads, and a namespace that does not end in / or #;

  • a container that is empty, a key that is neither type nor a compact or absolute IRI, a type that is not a list of IRIs, a value list that is empty or repeats a value, and a value that is null or a float with a fractional part;

  • a value object with both @language and @type, or with neither; an empty node; a node whose id is a blank-node label (_:b0);

  • an empty provenance, an entry of a subject without property or value (an entry of a flow-control element or a FlowMap names no pair), an empty beliefs, a belief without actor, belief or date, a belief, method or match outside its words, a property that is no IRI, and a date not written YYYY-MM-DD.

The statement rules run after every other rule and report their first problem, in this order: the context rule over every subject; the reserved keys of the manifest, the timelines and regions in subject order and the groups, each element’s keys in code-point order; then subject by subject in subject order, the manifest first, then root by root the timeline, its regions in array order and its children, depth first. For each subject they read its ID, its container’s keys in code-point order with their values, a value node where it stands, then its provenance entries, each entry’s keys in code-point order and its beliefs in list order, and last, entry by entry, the actors of its beliefs against each other and the entry against the earlier ones. After the last subject come the provenance entries about elements, those of the flow-control elements, root by root and each timeline’s before its children’s, then those of the FlowMaps: each entry’s keys in code-point order, and then, entry by entry, the actors of its beliefs against each other. Of the rules below, iri (a compact IRI), dangling_target, calendar_date and belief_actor apply to these entries.

An IRI fragment is a string of the characters A–Z, a–z, 0–9, -._~!$&'()*+,;=:@/?, code points from U+0080 on (white space excluded) and % followed by two hexadecimal digits. A reference that does not begin with a scheme and a colon and does not begin with # is a relative reference to another document and is not resolved. The # references of statements and provenance name any ID of the manifest: the manifest’s own, a timeline’s, a group’s, a MeasureMap’s, a conversion map’s at any depth, a region’s, a flow-control element’s, a resource’s, a FlowMap’s, a virtual timeline’s, a named timestamp’s, an elision’s, a named MatchStamp’s or an alignment anchor’s. The Gregorian calendar’s years run from 1 to 9999, so 2026-02-30 is refused. The messages are the same in both packages, but for a schema problem, whose message is the package’s own schema validator’s.

Warnings. A valid manifest may draw two warnings, which refuse nothing: context_absent at the root (the manifest has no @context) for any manifest without @context, and bundle_blank at statements, else at provenance (the manifest has statements but no ID; its subject is a blank node), for a manifest that states pairs or provenance without an id. Python’s manifest_warnings(value) and npm’s manifestWarnings(value) validate a manifest and return them as {code, path, message} entries, sorted by path and then by code. An unknown key inside a provenance entry is reported as an unknown key (README, “Unknown keys”), while the MatchClaim table, whose provenance column holds the entry’s members alone, refuses one (README, “Checking the tables”); @context, a container and its values are closed, and a key they do not declare is refused.

The statements alone. Python’s statement_problems(value) and npm’s statementProblems(value) check the statements of a manifest and nothing else, so that a library reading part of a manifest checks them without restating which other parts the rules read. They return a list of problems, empty where the statements are sound: first, member by member, the manifest’s @context and every statements and provenance of the manifest, a timeline or a region in subject order, each checked for a number that is not finite (finite_number), else against the schema (schema), each member’s first problem at its own path; then, only where no member has one, all the problems of the statement rules. They refuse nothing outside the statements and do not read the version marker.

13. The statement fixtures#

Four fixtures under fixtures/0.2.0/statements/, each with its minimal twin, carry statements; besides them, fixtures/0.2.0/flow/sonata_exposition states its three atomic sections, regions of the type tts:AtomicSection, each pair with an entry of method inferred, and three FlowMaps computed from its flow control, each with a provenance entry of method inferred about the FlowMap itself (README, “FlowMaps and virtual timelines”), and every other fixture holds the format’s context and no statement. Their structure is built by timetoalign and their statements and provenance are attached from scripts/fixtures/descriptions/<name>.yaml when they are emitted.

djset_segment
